## 0. Goal and scope: exactly two central phenomena

Develop **one parsimonious, mechanistic stochastic theory** that explains and predicts both of the following:

1. **Memory-dependent reversal of the effect of communication.** Increasing the number of posts an agent reads per activation, from `q` to `q + Δq`, **amplifies** false-controller manipulation when the agents' memory is sufficiently short but **protects** against manipulation when memory is sufficiently persistent. The crossover is a curve in the `(ρ, q)` plane, not an established thermodynamic phase transition. Its location differs between the `task003-symmetric` (distributed proof exists) and `task003-nosolution` (no proof exists) worlds. A successful model should predict the **sign, location, and broad magnitude** of the reading response and explain why the proof changes the boundary.

2. **Long-lived collective persistence of externally introduced information.** After the controller stops, its facts remain in agents' memories and in the readable blackboard through **rereading and reposting**, and the controller-induced population bias persists far longer than an unrefreshed individual fact. Stopping interaction destroys the effect rapidly; freezing the existing readable board preserves it; deleting controller-exclusive facts removes most of it and leaves a smaller effect associated with altered survival of agent-origin evidence. A successful model should predict the **persistence mechanism, its timescale or finite-horizon lower bound, and the outcomes of these interventions**.

**Do not pursue a collection of disconnected empirical fits.** The same state variables, acquisition/forgetting/posting laws, and Bayesian inference rule must account for both phenomena. The aim is an explanatory, predictive model that informs the design of robust multi-agent systems and is transferable, with controlled qualifications, to LLM-based agents.

**Established benchmark facts to reproduce and explain:**

- For fixed controller parameters (`q_c=12`, `λ_c=1`, `B=30`, `W=48`), the reading increment `D(ρ; q→q+3)` reverses sign. With a proof, the `3→6` crossover is bracketed around `ρ=0.5–0.625`; without a proof it occurs around `ρ=0.875–1`. Subsequent reading increments generally cross at lower `ρ`. Not all boundary cells are statistically resolved.
- With a proof, greater reading and persistent memory can eventually integrate a conclusive argument for `A0`; without a proof, perfect aggregation still leaves uncertainty. **The worlds also differ in available facts and controller pools; do not claim that proof availability has been causally isolated.**
- In normal post-controller evolution (`t=30→130`), susceptibility remains approximately `0.43→0.42` (proof, `ρ=.25`) and `0.30→0.29` (no proof, `ρ=.25`); it changes more slowly at `ρ=.75`. Controller-only facts still account for roughly 27–60% of readable posts; this reflects **agent reposting**, not passive retention of old controller posts.
- Blocking reading/posting causes fast decay; keeping the board frozen but readable maintains the effect; removing controller-exclusive facts from memories and the board destroys the dominant effect within about one agent-activation time. The remaining **initial belief-level** shift equals the indirect term algebraically, but later vote residuals need not equal it exactly.
- The new heatmaps validate `G_belief = G_direct + G_indirect`. At many crossovers the indirect increment overwhelms a positive direct increment; at others both terms become protective. **These are diagnostic attributions, not autonomous dynamical mechanisms.**
- Snapshot-belief increments and *last-recorded vote* increments agree in sign when both are statistically resolved, but systematically differ in magnitude. The last votes were cast from earlier, often fuller memories. Do not silently use snapshot belief as a quantitative surrogate for votes.
- Previous scans largely delimit the relevance of `q_c`, controller rate, budget, and `W` for these questions. **Audit what is available; do not rerun broad grids in these parameters.**

## 2. Why `G_direct` / `G_indirect` are not sufficient theoretical variables

For agent `i`, let `F_i^C` and `F_i^S` denote the agent-origin facts retained under controlled and silent dynamics, and let `H_i^C` denote controller-exclusive facts retained under control. With `p_2(M)=P(A2 | M)`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_{\mathrm{direct}} &= \mathbb E[p_2(F_i^C\cup H_i^C)-p_2(F_i^C)],\\
G_{\mathrm{indirect}} &= \mathbb E[p_2(F_i^C)-p_2(F_i^S)],\\
G_{\mathrm{belief}} &=G_{\mathrm{direct}}+G_{\mathrm{indirect}}.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is an exact **bookkeeping identity for posterior beliefs** (with an order-dependent allocation of interactions). It is scientifically useful for diagnosing manipulation, and after deleting `H_i^C`, its indirect term is precisely the **instantaneous counterfactual belief residual**.

However, neither term is a primitive mechanism:

- `G_direct` depends on **which controller facts are retained** *and* **how much those facts shift a posterior in the context of retained original evidence**. Its decline need not imply fewer controller facts circulating.
- `G_indirect` compares two changing sets of original facts; it is **not a pure measure of evidence integration**. It can rise or fall depending on the factual *content* retained under both arms, even if both arms integrate more evidence as `q` grows.
- Both increments can be positive or both negative; this is not paradoxical. The terms attribute the net response by **information provenance**, not by physical process.
- The `D_direct + D_indirect = 0` boundary at a reading reversal is an identity, not a predictive explanation of where that boundary lies.

**Likewise, stop searching for further post-hoc observables (new KLs, log-losses, fact counts, or alternate decompositions) unless a proposed equation demands a specific measurable input.** Another heatmap could re-label a phenomenon without explaining it. The correct next objective is a *generative mechanism* that predicts the pre-existing observables. Keep `G_direct`, `G_indirect`, fact-coverage, and the experimental interventions as **independent checks and interpretive outputs**, not tunable ingredients.

## 3. The proposed modeling principle: fact circulation coupled to evidence-dependent selection

Construct **one minimal mesoscopic stochastic model** with two coupled levels:

- **Propagation / storage:** fact-specific occupancy in agent memories and on the finite readable board, with reading, forgetting, and posting.
- **Inference / selection:** an agent's Bayesian posterior given its retained fact set determines the distribution of its vote; the vote and retained facts determine which fact it reposts. This feedback then changes propagation.

The model **must preserve, or explicitly approximate**, the simulator's crucial distinction between a proof-bearing fact combination and an inconclusive combination. Do not replace the world with additive evidence weights or an arbitrary sigmoid unless the approximation is checked against the original exact Bayesian posterior table: logical proofs and correlations between facts may be nonadditive.

### 3.1 Definitions and candidate equations

Let `\mathcal F` be the finite set of distinct factual contents. A single fact has identifier `f∈\mathcal F`. The following quantities are **not extra plotting targets**; they are candidate state variables required to write transition laws:

- `m_f(t)`: fraction of agents currently remembering fact `f`.
- `b_f(t)`: fraction of the `W` readable posts containing fact `f`.
- `\mu_t(M)`: distribution across agents of the full remembered fact set `M⊆\mathcal F`; approximations to `\mu_t` must be identified explicitly.
- `p_a(M)=P(Aa\mid M)`: the simulator's exact Bayesian posterior for answer `Aa`, with `a∈{0,1,2}`.
- `\pi_f(M)`: probability that an activated agent with post-reading memory `M` publishes fact `f`, **computed from the actual probability-matching vote and fact-selection rule** (zero if `f∉M`).
- `s_f(t)=\sum_M\mu_t^{\mathrm{postread}}(M)\pi_f(M)`: probability the next agent post contains `f`. The superscript `postread` means immediately after the current activation's reading operation.
- `\gamma=-\ln\rho`: continuous-time unrefreshed fact-forgetting rate, provided this matches the implemented lifetime law.
- `\tau_b≈W/N`: time for the readable board to turn over when each of `N` agents posts on average once per unit time, neglecting controller posts.

As a **starting approximation**, for rare or independently sampled board entries,

\[
r_f(q,b_f)=1-(1-b_f)^q
\]

is the probability that `q` reads encounter fact `f`. Check whether simulator sampling is with or without replacement, excludes one's own posts, and can select multiple identical facts. If without replacement and exactly `n_f` readable posts contain `f`, use

\[
r_f=1-\frac{\binom{W-n_f}{q}}{\binom{W}{q}}
\]

for an eligible board with `W` posts; adjust eligibility for self-post exclusion.

A first, explicitly **mean-field**, dynamics is

\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\dot m_f &\simeq r_f(q,b_f)(1-m_f)-\gamma m_f,\\
\tau_b\dot b_f &\simeq s_f(\mu_t,q)-b_f.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{M1}
\]

Explain every closure assumption and when it fails. The actual board is a **sliding FIFO window of posts**, not an exponential-loss reservoir; the corresponding finite-window/age-structured relation is a candidate correction. Before controller termination, an illustrative mixed-reservoir equation is

\[
W\dot b_f \simeq N s_f + u_f - (N+u_{\mathrm{tot}})b_f,
\]

where `u_f(t)` is the controller's **actual expected rate of posting fact `f`** under its rate, vote gate, proof stop, and remaining budget, and `u_tot(t)=Σ_f u_f(t)` is its total expected posting rate. Derive or simulate `u_f` using the specified controller policy; using the logged controller event stream is permissible **only in a diagnostic conditional-on-input test**, not as a substitute for a self-consistent predictive model. Set `u_f=0` after `t=30`. **Do not use `G`, `D`, or the long-time susceptibility curves as input functions.**

Critically, **(M1) is not yet a complete theory**: `s_f` must be derived from, or constrained by, the actual vote-and-fact-selection rule and an approximation to the distribution of *jointly* remembered evidence `\mu_t(M)`. A pure collection of independent one-fact occupancies may fail to represent the simultaneous collection of complementary facts needed for a proof. This is the main modeling challenge.

### 3.2 Minimal sequence of model construction

1. **Derive exact microscopic transition ingredients from source code.** Record activation rate; board sampling law; forgetting/reset law; what an agent knows before/after reading; the exact Bayes posterior function; probability-matching vote; tie-breaking and repost selection; controller posts and gate. Document every assumption rather than inventing it.
2. **Implement the smallest transparent closure** for `\mu_t(M)` that allows the Bayesian posting expectation `s_f` to be predicted, not measured and inserted at each time. Begin with fact-marginal / independence closure **only as a falsifiable baseline**. If it fails, add the minimum necessary correlations or coarse-grained evidence states (for example, an indicator for particular complementary fact sets or posterior classes); justify complexity by demonstrably improved out-of-sample predictions.
3. **Predict actual voting, not only contemporaneous beliefs.** Because recorded votes are from the last agent activation, an appropriate approximation includes a lagged-vote state `x_a(t)`, e.g.
   \[
   \dot x_a\simeq \nu\big(\bar p_a^{\mathrm{at\ vote}}(t)-x_a\big),\quad\nu=1,
   \]
   where `\bar p_a^{at vote}` is the expected posterior **after reading at an activation**. Verify this equation from the Poisson update rule. This avoids the known systematic error from comparing last votes with post-forgetting memory snapshots.
4. **Keep the two original worlds separate but use the same physical rules.** The task-specific input is the factual world / Bayesian posterior function, not separate ad hoc signs or fitted crossover curves.
5. **Analyze local stability and finite-size persistence.** For rare fact `f`, linearize (M1) under a justified approximation `s_f≈κ_f m_f`. This suggests a diagnostic reproduction factor `\mathcal R_f≈q\kappa_f/\gamma`, but **do not assert a universal threshold**: `κ_f` depends on the evidence context and competing facts, the `ρ=1` limit needs separate treatment, and finite populations can lose facts through extinction. Derive eigenvalues or extinction estimates only for a justified closure; distinguish a stable equilibrium, slow mode, metastability, and eventual absorption.
6. **Show mathematically how the sign reversal arises.** Vary `ρ` and `q` in the model and calculate `D(ρ;q_1→q_2)` *without feeding the observed `D` into the model*. Attribute the result to how reading modifies fact exposure, the composition of jointly remembered evidence, and the evidence-dependent likelihood of reposting. If the model cannot predict the reversal, it has not explained the main phenomenon, regardless of how accurately it fits fact persistence.
7. **Obtain analytical results, not just numerical agreement.** Aim to prove at least one nontrivial proposition under clearly stated reduced-model assumptions, for example a local persistence/extinction stability criterion or a sufficient condition for the communication response to change sign. Separately derive the elementary absorbing-belief property of a remembered, conclusive proof at `ρ=1`; do not mistake this simple observation for a theorem about the entire interacting population. State which results depend on independence, mean-field, and/or large-`N` limits. If no useful theorem emerges, explain exactly why and provide testable analytical inequalities or asymptotics instead.

**Model-selection rule:** Prefer a model with a few identifiable, code-grounded quantities and a successful joint prediction to a highly flexible fit. A model that simply reproduces the original simulator one-for-one is a useful numerical benchmark, **not** an explanatory reduced theory. State model dimensionality, fitted quantities, and what was genuinely derived.

## 4. Focused analysis and validation against existing Simulation 2 data

### 4.1 First: audit and pre-register train/validation splits

Before calibrating anything, create a concise data inventory: episode IDs, `ρ`, `q`, agent memories, original/controller fact IDs, readable board content, individual post timestamps and authors, controller-post events, last-vote timestamps/votes, and any per-action posterior logs. Mark unavailable variables clearly. Validate randomized streams and reproduce existing primary figures. **No new runs at this stage.**

Freeze a **calibration subset** and a **held-out validation subset** *before* adjusting closure complexity. For example, estimate transition/posting parameters from observed microscopic events in a limited set of `ρ,q` conditions and silent/controller trajectories, then predict the unused `ρ` values, `q=9` if not calibrated, a second world, and post-controller times/branches. Do not tune on the complete `(ρ,q)` heatmap and then claim its reproduction is predictive validation.

### 4.2 Quantitative checks (in scientific priority order)

**A. Mechanistic closure check:** Compare model-predicted versus measured (not fitted time series of) `m_f`, `b_f`, fact diversity/coverage, and the distribution of informative joint fact combinations. Calibrate any unavoidable closure parameters at the event or transition level; report uncertainty and held-out error. Use controller-only fact IDs and original evidence fact IDs, not just pooled mean counts. The existing records likely suffice for most checks; identify any missing exact post/reading event only when needed.

**B. Reading reversal (phenomenon 1):** Predict, for each world and `ρ∈{.125,.25,.375,.5,.625,.75,.875,1}`, the vote-based

\[
G_t(\rho,q)=\mathbb E[x^{C}_{A2}(t)-x^{S}_{A2}(t)],\quad
D_t(\rho;q_1\to q_2)=G_t(\rho,q_2)-G_t(\rho,q_1),
\]

at `t=30`, for the three observed increments `3→6`, `6→9`, `9→12`. Predict the signs, qualitative magnitudes, non-monotonic optimum in `q`, and shift between the two worlds. Overlay model outputs on the **existing** confidence-interval heatmaps. As secondary diagnostics, calculate `G_direct`/`G_indirect` *from predicted fact-set distributions* and compare with the existing decomposition—not vice versa.

**C. Persistence and intervention branches (phenomenon 2):** Starting from the controlled and silent populations at `t=30`, use the **same model parameters** to predict the post-stop paired susceptibility `δ(t)` through `t=130` for `ρ=.25` and `.75`, both worlds, under all four archived rules:

- **Normal:** controller absent, agents read and post.
- **No reading or posting:** no new acquisition or messages; memories can decay and votes update under the stated simulator rule.
- **Frozen readable board:** no new posts, but agents can repeatedly read the `t=30` snapshot.
- **Controller-only-fact removal:** erase those facts from all agent memories and readable posts at `t=30`; retain original facts and permit subsequent normal evolution.

Predict `m_f(t)`, `b_f(t)`, and `δ(t)` together. In particular, explain near-constant `δ` under normal and frozen-board conditions, its fast loss when interaction stops, and its collapse to an indirect-like residual after removal. Treat the latter as a **belief-level instantaneous identity plus a time-dependent vote-level prediction**, not an automatic equality at `t=40` or `t=130`.

**D. Robustness / held-out variants:** If the model passes A–C, use *already archived* `W=24/240` and `B=100` runs as additional held-out tests. Explain why the longer board window often protects, why larger budgets matter more in the proof world, and what the model gets wrong. Do not require perfect quantitative agreement with every controller-rate point as a condition for progress.

### 4.3 Success and iteration criteria

A theory is **not satisfactory** if it merely fits `G(t)` phenomenologically, correctly predicts persistence but not the communication sign change, reproduces sign changes only after world-specific ad hoc sign parameters, or assumes independently remembered facts where proof structure is essential.

A satisfactory first theory must:

- Predict **both** the memory-dependent signs/crossover ordering of `D` and persistence/removal behavior with **one set of transition rules**; quantify mistakes at resolved cells and the uncertainties at unresolved ones.
- Predict at least a substantial majority of statistically resolved reading-response signs on genuinely held-out conditions, including **both positive and negative** examples in each relevant world (with exact fractions and exceptions reported). Aim to bracket crossover boundaries within one tested `ρ` increment (0.125) when data support such resolution; do not fabricate exact critical values.
- Predict the **ordering and approximate persistence/decay scales** of the four post-stop conditions, without fitting a separate relaxation curve for each condition; provide residual plots and errors.
- Demonstrate an explanatory reduction: the model's state/parameterization is smaller or more structured than running the original detailed agent simulator, and its quantities map to reading, memory, posting, and evidence rules.
- Clearly report when a proposed closure fails. **Iterate deliberately**: diagnose which assumption failed, change **one mechanism or closure at a time**, repeat calibration on the same training split, and re-evaluate the untouched hold-out. Preserve previous versions and comparison tables. Do not proliferate phenomenological coefficients or request new simulations to rescue an untestable model.

If no compact model meets these goals, document the obstruction (e.g. fact correlations, posterior nonadditivity, finite-window feedback) and deliver the best falsifiable partial theory plus a precise next mathematical step. **Do not declare success by lowering the criteria after inspecting the results.**

## 5. One theory-derived defense: de-duplicate evidence exposure

**Working hypothesis:** Repetition on the board gives already-popular facts more chances to be read, reinforcing controller-origin messages. Reduce this *frequency advantage* while keeping the underlying factual content and read budget unchanged.

Implement **one** no-oracle defense: when an agent reads `q` posts from the newest `W`, sample so as to prioritize **distinct fact identities rather than duplicate posts containing the same fact**. A concrete first implementation is to choose fact IDs uniformly among currently readable distinct facts, without repeating a fact until the available distinct IDs have been exhausted; read a corresponding eligible post for each chosen ID. Specify behavior if fewer than `q` distinct eligible IDs exist. Retain `q`, `W`, memory rule, agent posting rate, controller strategy, and initial world. Do **not** use labels such as "controller-origin", the correct answer, or privileged knowledge in the operational defense. In an LLM version, deduplication may use canonical fact IDs or verifiable semantic equivalence; track errors separately.

**This is a falsifiable proposal, not a guaranteed improvement.** It may also expose previously rare controller facts, and could fail, especially in the no-proof world. The theory should predict *where* deduplication helps, hurts, or has little effect **before running the targeted defense comparison**.

Minimum test: in both worlds, compare standard versus deduplicated reading with paired seeds for a **small, preselected** set of regimes (one clearly amplifying and one clearly protective reading regime; e.g. `ρ=.25` and `.75` for proof, `ρ=.25` and `1` for no-proof, with `q=3,6`; adapt only if theoretical results justify). Include silent arms under each sampling rule. Report controlled correct-vote share, paired false-controller susceptibility, baseline accuracy, prevalence/coverage of original and controller facts, and persistence after stopping control. Equalize total reads, write rate, window, and controller opportunity. A defense that makes agents generally uninformed or worse under silence is **not** a successful defense merely because it lowers susceptibility.

If the defense fails, diagnose against the model and report the failure. Do not silently try a series of ad hoc alternative defenses. Propose **one** mechanistically justified revision for explicit review if needed.

## 6. Minimal transfer test to LLM-based agents

**Do this after a theory with honest held-out Simulation 2 validation exists.** The key test is whether the two phenomena survive outside exact Bayesian reasoning and, if so, whether the theory identifies which approximations break.

- Keep **the same task worlds, factual content, shared board, controller policy, and fact-selection/communication schedule** insofar as technically possible. Preserve controlled/silent pairing.
- Use **externally enforced memory**: explicitly provide each LLM its current remembered facts and only the `q` sampled posts per activation; forget facts according to the experimental `ρ`. Avoid accidentally giving the LLM its complete conversation or hidden prior posts. Preserve source/fact provenance in the experimental logs without giving agents privileged labels.
- Record selected answer, posted factual content/ID, visible memory, sampled posts, and (if reliable) the LLM's stated answer probabilities or confidence. Distinguish faithful fact reproduction, paraphrase, omission, and fabricated content. The exact Bayesian model is the comparator, not an assumption about LLM behavior.
- **Minimal reversal test:** use `q=3` and `6` with a strongly amplifying and a strongly protective `ρ` regime in **each world**, chosen from the validated sign table. Suggested: proof `ρ=.25` versus `.75`; no-proof `ρ=.25` versus `1`. Run silent and false-controller arms; use enough paired episodes to estimate uncertainty and avoid treating a single trajectory as evidence. Pilot briefly for API cost, token accounting, output quality, and robust fact-ID tracking before preregistering the confirmatory sample size.
- **Minimal persistence test:** select one short-memory setting that displays an intervention effect; continue after the controller stops and include normal continuation plus controller-exclusive fact removal. Add the frozen-board or interaction-stop branches only if needed to distinguish competing mechanisms in LLMs. Measure information survival and susceptibility, not just whether one memorable phrase persists.
- **Defense transfer:** test the same deduplicated-reading policy without task-specific redesign, under a matched token/read budget, if the baseline LLM experiment exhibits an effect worth mitigating.

Report whether LLMs reproduce the signs and persistence, how often they integrate the proof when it is available, and whether deviations from Bayesian predictions correspond to evidence-handling/selection failures. **Do not promise that LLM agents will show either phenomenon.** If memory is not genuinely controlled or facts cannot be reliably traced, the experiment cannot establish the mechanism.

## 7. Optional finite-size program: `N=12, 240, 2400`

Investigate finite-size effects **only after** the model predicts the baseline mechanisms and provides a concrete scaling hypothesis. The main targets are (i) whether the sign-change curve sharpens, shifts, or vanishes with `N`, and (ii) whether the apparent collective lifetime reflects a true stable state or a metastable fact-circulation process whose extinction time grows with population size.

**Avoid trivial scaling artifacts:** if agent activity is one per unit time each, the aggregate post rate is proportional to `N`. To preserve comparable board visibility and controller pressure, a first scaling convention is

\[
W_N=48\,(N/24),\quad \lambda_{c,N}=1\,(N/24),\quad B_N=30\,(N/24),
\]

with rounding where needed, `q` and `q_c` unchanged, and time measured in activations per agent. Pre-register the choice; an additional fixed-absolute-controller-budget experiment would be a *different* question, not finite-size scaling under equivalent exposure. At `N=2400`, feasibility and the controller gate / rate implementation must be checked before any long run.

**The difficult part is the task world, not only the clocks.** Current proof-bearing world has 18 distinct agent-origin facts initially distributed across 24 agents at one fact per agent. With `N=12`, one fact per agent **cannot contain the same 18 distinct facts**. Likewise, duplicating the same 24-agent seed across `N=240/2400` increases initial redundancy and changes extinction probabilities. Therefore:

1. First propose a documented family of world/initialization schemes that preserves the epistemic structure (existence or absence of a collective proof, controller pool informativeness, prior/evidence strength, and per-agent information budget) as far as possible. Explicitly identify unavoidable differences. **Do not treat `N=12` as an exactly matched copy of the 24-agent world.**
2. If exact one-fact-per-agent comparability is essential, build an appropriately scaled 12-agent fact world and demonstrate comparable collective evidence structure; for `N=240/2400`, distinguish fixed-fact-pool replication from a fact-pool-size-growing family. Alternatively use a matched cloned-agent series at multiples of 24 for a clean control, while reporting `N=12` separately.
3. For each valid scaling family, run a **small** preselected set of `ρ,q`: one amplifying, one protective, and at most one near the modeled crossover, plus normal/removal persistence branches. Estimate confidence intervals for the sign, crossover position, fact-extinction probabilities, and `δ(t)` decay or censoring. Finite observation horizons provide lower bounds, not infinite lifetimes.
4. Compare stochastic finite-`N` measurements to the theory's deterministic limit and fluctuation/extinction predictions. Do not call a smooth crossover a phase transition without an independently justified limiting argument and finite-size scaling evidence.

Treat `N=2400` as a **conditional milestone**: estimate computational cost, run a short pilot, and proceed only if the earlier sizes reveal a testable scaling law. Provide a go/no-go note rather than launching a large run automatically.

## 8. Working method, organization, and final deliverables

### Research workflow

1. **Inventory** existing experiments/code; register data limitations and hold-out splits; reproduce the primary baseline measurements.
2. **Derive and implement** minimal theory M1 plus the simplest legitimate evidence-dependent posting closure. Record analytical approximations and their failure modes.
3. **Iterate on the theory**, changing one assumption at a time and preserving a model-version log. Do not seek new observables unless they are required to discriminate between explicitly competing equations.
4. **Validate** the two central phenomena against held-out existing data, then all four persistence intervention branches. Compare both worlds with the same physical rules.
5. **Only then**, perform the smallest justified new tests: deduplicated-reading defense, LLM transfer, and optional `N` scaling, in that order if resources permit.
6. **Write the scientific report** and an executive summary of supported claims, unsupported claims, and decisive failures. No speculative claims of a phase transition or permanent attractor from finite-time curves.

### Required repository structure (adapt names to project conventions)

Keep separate folders for `theory/`, `calibration/`, `held_out_validation/`, `defense/`, `llm/`, `finite_size/`, and `reports/` with versioned configurations, seeds, code, tables and plots. Never overwrite original simulation data; record code versions/commits and all assumptions. Existing outputs can be linked rather than copied. Maintain a concise `progress.md` recording hypothesis, experiment, outcome, and next decision; avoid redundant full parameter sweeps.

### Required main report: `theory_and_validation_report.md` (+ figures and code)

The final report must be self-contained and include:

1. **Exact statement of the two phenomena** and the baseline empirical constraints.
2. **Microscopic rules, minimal state variables, derivation of the mesoscopic model**, closure assumptions, and analytical predictions (reading-reversal condition; information-reproduction/relaxation mechanism; limitations).
3. **Calibration protocol and pre-registered hold-outs:** fitted quantities, data provenance, identifiability, uncertainty, and reproducibility.
4. **Quantitative theory-versus-data comparisons** in both worlds: `D` sign maps, crossover brackets, `G(t)` / `δ(t)` trajectories, controller-fact memory/board prevalence, all four post-stop interventions, and diagnostic direct/indirect predictions. Include failure cases and ablations of model assumptions.
5. **Decision on theoretical adequacy:** what the model predicts without fitting, what remains phenomenological, why alternative closures were rejected, and what minimal new test would distinguish remaining explanations.
6. **Defense and LLM results** when completed, with comparable budgets and uncertainty; optional finite-size section only when scientifically interpretable.
7. **Clear practical conclusions** for engineering swarms resistant to manipulation, tied to demonstrated mechanisms rather than mere association.

**Iteration is explicitly requested:** continue refining the theory and testing its held-out predictions until it meets the standards in §4.3, or until a precise, documented barrier has been established. Keep theory construction the central activity throughout; do not displace it with ever more descriptive diagnostics. If a larger simulation campaign is truly necessary, first submit a brief justification naming the missing causal distinction, the smallest test that resolves it, and the expected decision it will inform.
