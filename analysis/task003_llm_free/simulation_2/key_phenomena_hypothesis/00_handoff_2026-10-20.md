## 0. Executive instructions to the agent

There are **two immediate research priorities**, to be pursued as parts of **one coherent study**, comparing proof-bearing and no-proof tasks:

1. **Memory-dependent reversal in the effect of communication.** Increasing agent reading `q` sometimes **reduces** a false controller's influence and sometimes **increases** it. Map the reversal in the `(rho, q)` plane, and explain it through competition between integrating complementary evidence and reinforcing selectively propagated evidence. Test how the structure/strength of collective evidence moves the boundary.
2. **Long-lived collective informational states.** A controller-induced shift in population votes persists long after its interventions stop, even though individual facts and readable board posts have much shorter unreinforced lifetimes. Measure what actually persists, for how long, and whether it depends on ongoing communication and controller-origin facts.

**Do not begin by launching comprehensive sweeps.** First inventory and analyze all existing episodes, notebooks, logs, outputs, and figure-generating code. Determine precisely what is already resolved and what cannot be answered without new instrumentation or runs. **Recommend at most two new experimental campaigns**, described in Sections 6 and 7, and make both targeted/adaptive rather than full grids. Do not rerun `q_c`, controller-rate, vote-gate, budget, or board-window grids by default. Investigate the evidence already in those grids before concluding that anything needs to be rerun.

A third observed phenomenon, **saturation or backfire of intervention intensity**, is potentially interesting but **secondary**. Do not elevate it to a separate major campaign or independent paper before the two priorities are understood.

## 2. Model: concise but technically accurate

### 2.1 Shared simulator

- `N=24` agents; asynchronous random activations, on average **one activation per agent per unit time**.
- Each agent starts with one true fact. On activation it samples `q` posts from the `W` most recent *readable* posts, excluding its own; incorporates the facts into a memory; performs **exact Bayesian inference** over `A0` (correct), `A1`, `A2`; **probability-matches** its vote; and posts one remembered fact supporting its vote.
- A remembered fact survives one time unit with probability `rho`. Reading it again renews its lifetime. Forgetting is at the **fact** level, not simply forgetting the vote.
- The common board retains all historical posts in storage, but only the **latest `W`** posts are eligible for reading. This is a **moving readability window**, not physical deletion of old messages.
- A false controller targets `A2`; a truth controller targets `A0`. Controller opportunities occur at rate `lambda_c`; it inspects `q_c` agent posts and selects one true fact from its pool supporting its target. A **75% vote gate** may silence it. The controller is limited to `B` successful posts and operates at most until `t=30`; agents evolve without it until `t=40` in published runs. The proof-bearing case also has a proof stop.
- Three arms (silent, controller A0, controller A2) share episode randomness for matched comparison. **Do not assume** random draws are paired *across q values, tasks, or other settings* until verified from code.
- Default: `q=6`, `q_c=12`, `lambda_c=1`, `B=30`, `W=48`, gate on. Baseline sweeps use `q in {3,6,12}`, `rho in {1,.75,.5,.25}` (and eight memory values available for no-proof baseline), multiple `lambda_c`, `q_c`, and gate settings, with 1,000 episodes per setting.

### 2.2 Two task families—not a one-factor causal comparison

| Feature | Proof-bearing (`task003-symmetric`) | No-proof (`task003-nosolution`) |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct initial agent facts | 18 | 15 |
| Posterior given **all** agents' initial facts | `(1, 0, 0)` | `(0.5, 0, 0.5)` |
| Controller fact pools | Separate 12-fact pools for A0 and A2, 10 facts new to agents in either pool | Same 12-fact pool for either target, all 12 new to agents |
| Posterior given agents' facts **plus** controller pool | `(1, 0, 0)` | `(0.75, 0, 0.25)` |
| Conclusive proof available? | Yes, distributed over agents | No, not even given all available facts |

**Key caution:** Changing task family changes *more* than proof existence: initial fact sets, controller pools, collective posterior, and possibly evidence redundancy/salience change together. Treat differences as **comparative evidence**, not isolated causal effects of proof availability. A later carefully matched family of worlds may be needed, but is **not** an immediate full experiment campaign.

### 2.3 Main observable

For the false controller, analyze **paired susceptibility**

\[
G(t;\rho,q,\ldots)=\mathbb E\!\left[x_{A2}^{\mathrm{ctrl}}(t)-x_{A2}^{\mathrm{silent}}(t)\right],
\]

where `x_{A2}(t)` is the fraction of agents whose latest vote is A2 (among agents who have voted). The silent and controlled runs belong to the same randomized episode. Preserve paired episode-level differences and compute uncertainty appropriately.

The reports also contain `I(Z;X)` (readability of controller target from a randomly chosen vote) and `eta=I(Z;X)/C` based on target-conditioned KL divergences. These largely follow susceptibility (for `I`) or stay approximately unchanged (for `eta`), and **are not the two current research priorities**. Avoid confusing this report-specific `eta` with a goal-directed per-message control efficiency or the separately discussed informed-versus-sham formulation.

## 3. Priority I: reversal of the effect of reading

### 3.1 Findings already established

At `t=30, lambda_c=1, q_c=12, W=48, B=30`, false-controller susceptibility is:

| `rho` | With proof: `G(q=3), G(q=6), G(q=12)` | Without proof: `G(q=3), G(q=6), G(q=12)` |
|---|---|---|
| `1` | `0.00, 0.00, 0.00` | `0.19, 0.14, 0.11` |
| `.75` | `.24, .11, .04` | `.22, .25, .23` |
| `.5` | `.28, .33, .18` | `.20, .29, .30` |
| `.25` | `.24, .43, .40` | `.16, .30, .37` |

**Interpretation of endpoint difference:** `D_3->12(rho) = G(rho,12)-G(rho,3)` is respectively `-.20, -.10, +.16` with proof for `rho=.75,.5,.25`, and `-.08,+.01,+.10,+.21` without proof for `rho=1,.75,.5,.25`. The no-proof `+.01` is very small and may be within uncertainty; do **not** treat its sign as statistically established without revisiting raw paired/independent sampling.

- **With proof:** increased reading strongly protects at `rho=.75`, amplifies susceptibility at `rho=.25`, and is **non-monotone at `rho=.5`**: `q=3->6` increases `G`, while `q=6->12` decreases it. Thus, there is **not necessarily a single vertical phase boundary** `rho_c=0.5`.
- **Without proof:** at `rho=1` increased reading protects (`G=.19->.11`), even though no proof can be reconstructed; at low memory increased reading amplifies susceptibility. Thus, **literal proof reconstruction is not a necessary condition** for protective reading.
- The apparent change in the endpoint sign occurs at **different memory values** in the two worlds. With proof, positive at `.25`, negative by `.5`; without proof, negative at `1`, near zero at `.75`, positive at `.5`. This is a **hypothesis about a shifted boundary**, not proof of a sharp transition.
- At `rho=1`, a remembered conclusive proof produces an absorbing **epistemic** state for that agent: if `P(A0|S)=1`, then consistent true additional evidence preserves it. No such proof state exists in the no-proof case; even all available evidence leaves `P(A0)=.75, P(A2)=.25`.

**Different crossings must not be conflated:** The proof-bearing baseline Fig. 1 shows the **false-controller A2 vote majority** emerging between `rho=.75` and `.5` for `q=6`; that majority threshold is **not** the sign boundary of `D_3->12`. `rho=.5` also has a simple timescale interpretation (see below), but current data do not show an exact critical value or a thermodynamic phase transition.

### 3.2 Mechanism to test (hypothesis, not established result)

Reading performs two competing functions:

1. **Evidence integration:** agents encounter diverse complementary facts that correct selection bias; with persistent memory they can accumulate enough evidence to become confident or, in the proof task, certain.
2. **Selective reinforcement:** the controller injects a biased **selection of true facts**; agents subsequently vote and post facts supporting their votes, potentially making that selection self-reinforcing. When memory is short, increasing reading accelerates exposure to the biased circulating sample without enabling sufficient complementary evidence to coexist in memory.

The proof task intensifies the integrative side because multiple facts can establish certainty. The no-proof result shows the integrative side cannot be defined solely by the binary event "proof reconstructed." Use **posterior movement**, **distinct complementary fact coverage**, **posterior entropy/odds**, and **memory diversity** in *both* tasks.

A heuristic worth testing (not a fitted theory): if fact `f` represents fraction `b_f` of readable posts, an agent reading `q` posts has per-activation exposure probability approximately `r_f(q)=1-(1-b_f)^q` under a simple sampling approximation. With forgetting hazard `gamma=-ln(rho)`, a crude occupancy approximation is `m_f ~ r_f/(r_f+gamma)`. Simultaneously holding `K` complementary facts may scale roughly like a product of occupancies, whereas exposure to a few controller-favoring facts may need only a few factors. Independence assumptions are generally false in the simulator; **do not claim this is a derivation**.

### 3.3 Observable to characterize the reversal

For discrete reading counts use actual **finite differences**, not an unqualified continuous derivative:

\[
D_{q_1\rightarrow q_2}(\rho)
=G(30;\rho,q_2)-G(30;\rho,q_1),\qquad q_2>q_1.
\]

Compute separately for `3->6` and `6->12`, and then only if necessary for finer local `q` intervals. A sign change of `D` defines an **operational crossover contour** in the `(rho,q)` grid. Avoid "phase transition" terminology without system-size scaling or a theoretical singular limit.

Find out whether that contour tracks posterior certainty/confidence, diversity/coverage of agent-original facts, the fraction proving A0 (proof task only), and the share of currently readable/remembered controller-exclusive or controller-amplified facts.

## 4. Priority II: collective memory beyond individual fact lifetimes

### 4.1 Distinguish three timescales

If fact survival after elapsed time `t` is `rho^t=e^{-gamma t}`, with `gamma=-ln rho`:

\[
\tau_{\rm individual}^{\rm unreinforced}=\frac{1}{-\ln\rho},\qquad
 t_{1/2}^{\rm individual}=\frac{\ln2}{-\ln\rho},\qquad
\tau_{\rm board}\approx\frac{W}{24}.
\]

For `rho=.75,.5,.25`, unreinforced individual mean fact lifetimes are approximately `3.48,1.44,.72` time units, and half-lives `2.41,1,.5`. At `rho=.5`, **the memory half-life equals the mean inter-activation time** (one time unit). This makes `.5` a natural crossover *scale*, but gives no universal theorem fixing a transition exactly there. At `W=24,48,240`, approximate readable post lifetimes are `1,2,10` units (assuming about 24 agent posts per unit time; extra posts, eligibility and stochastic turnover complicate this).

A fact may live **much longer than the unreinforced lifetime if repeatedly reread**. Thus an individual unreinforced lifetime is not the measured residence time of that fact under interaction. The collective lifetime must be independently defined and estimated.

### 4.2 Evidence to date

- With proof, for `rho<1` and numerous settings, `G` is nearly constant over `t=30->40` after controller termination; in the baseline the report says changes are at most about `.02`.
- Without proof, susceptibility persists too; the baseline report says its change over `t=30->40` is at most about `.03` for studied settings. Hence persistence **does not require that a proof existed and was destroyed**.
- Even with `W=24` and `rho=.25`—where unreinforced fact memory is `~.72` and an un-reposted message is readable for `~1` unit—substantial susceptibility is still visible at `t=40`. Persistence of the *population effect* is longer than straightforward passive persistence of an unreinforced individual fact or isolated message.
- A longer window protects the proof-bearing task appreciably, but does not replace long individual memory. In the proof task at `rho=.5, q=6, lambda_c=1`, A2 vote share at `t=30` is `.60,.54,.43` for `W=24,48,240`. In no-proof it is `.81,.77,.73`: the window effect is considerably smaller.
- The proof-bearing baseline shows near-collapse of individuals holding a proof: at `t=30` the silent share proving A0 is about `100%,12%,1%,0%` for `rho=1,.75,.5,.25`, although A0 vote shares are substantially higher. The no-proof task has **no proof-retention observable**; use confidence and evidence composition instead.

### 4.3 Critical distinction: persistence of *what*?

Current evidence directly shows a persistent **population-level intervention-induced vote difference**, not yet a measured intrinsic relaxation time or definite ongoing circulation of controller-origin facts.

Separate:

- **Fact persistence:** exact controller-exclusive or controller-origin facts continue to be remembered/reposted, potentially via repeated agent-to-agent renewal.
- **State persistence:** the intervention biases which *agent-original* facts agents select and repost; the population keeps a biased belief/vote state even after controller-exclusive facts cease to circulate.
- **Long transient vs stationary history dependence:** a finite-duration apparent plateau might be a very slow transient, metastability, or a distinct long-lived/absorbing communication state. Determine which before assigning a single `tau_collective`.

Define the ensemble response relative to an appropriately paired silent arm:

\[
\delta(t)=E[x_{A2}^{\rm ctrl}(t)-x_{A2}^{\rm silent}(t)].
\]

For each episode record `t_*`, the **actual last successful controller post**, and also the nominal cutoff `t=30`; fast controllers can exhaust their budgets much earlier. Compare post-intervention histories aligned to `t_*`, with appropriate episode censoring/conditioning, while retaining fixed-clock `delta(t)` for unbiased arm comparisons. Beware of selection bias if comparing only episodes with an early or late last post.

A practical nonparametric decay time is the **half-response time** after intervention cessation, e.g. first `s` for which `|delta(t_*+s)|` falls to half its initial magnitude, with bootstrap uncertainty and **right-censoring** if it never occurs. Because `t_*` is episode-specific, define the paired ensemble alignment carefully and check that the silent arm receives the same clock transformation. If `delta` tends to a nonzero plateau `delta_inf`, a fit should instead consider `delta(t)-delta_inf`; do **not** estimate a relaxation time from an uncorrected plateau. Only fit an exponential law when the data substantiate it.

A hypothetical exponential time constant from two time points is
`- (t2-t1) / ln[delta(t2)/delta(t1)]`, but it is **not a measured result from the available plots**. In particular, do not infer a numerical value of `tau_collective` from an almost flat curve without data precision and model checks.

## 5. What existing parameter sweeps already tell us—avoid redoing them

The existing report variants are not distractions: they constrain mechanisms and tell us where **not** to spend simulation time.

### 5.1 Budget and rate

- Proof task: raising `B=30 ->100` permits fast controllers to continue posting; at `rho=.5, q=6, lambda_c=8`, false-controller `G(t=30)` rises roughly `.37 -> .50` (about 29 vs 78 posts). Thus the earlier near-saturation with increasing `lambda_c` was partly a **budget cap effect**, not an intrinsic lack of responsiveness.
- No-proof task: the same change leads roughly `.29 -> .30` at `rho=.5, q=6, lambda_c=8`, despite far more messages (at rate 8, the larger-budget runs post about 65–91 rather than 26–29 across memory conditions). Apparent **evidence/dynamical saturation** is much stronger here.
- No-proof full-memory (`rho=1`) exhibits **backfire of rate/intensity** for target A2: at `q=6, t=30`, `G` falls from around `.20` at `lambda_c=.5` to `.05` at rate 8 with `B=30`, and approximately `.02` with `B=100`. A plausible (untested) mechanism is that fast dissemination increases the number of distinct facts agents integrate, pulling beliefs toward the full-evidence posterior favoring A0.
- Existing `lambda_c` and `B` sweeps are **adequate for describing this contrast**. Do not rerun those grids for priorities I/II unless the data audit exposes a specific missing causal measurement. If possible, use existing logs to plot `G` against delivered messages and **distinct delivered facts**, not just nominal rate.

### 5.2 Board-window effects

- Proof task: at `rho<=.5`, `W=240` reduces `G` by roughly `.06-.14` compared with `W=48`, and `W=24` can increase it by up to `.09`; report attributes possible protection to retaining older complementary agent facts (hypothesis, not verified).
- No-proof task: window effects are smaller, generally about `.02-.06` reduction for longer `W`, or up to `.03` increase for shorter `W`.
- The **reading reversal persists** across all three window values in both tasks.
- Increasing `W` also changes the controller's `q_c`-based view of older votes and its gate. The variants are **not a pure board-memory intervention**. Do not infer causality from `W` alone without checking posting counts/gate effects; nevertheless, no additional `W` sweep is currently justified.

### 5.3 Controller sensing `q_c` and gate

- Proof task at `rho=.5, q=6`: report Fig. 6 gives mean `G` across rates around `.28,.33,.34,.31` for `q_c=3,6,12,24`—modest and not clearly monotonic.
- No-proof task at `rho=.5, q=6`: report Fig. 6 gives corresponding means around `.30,.28,.29,.30`; the effect is negligible for these conditions.
- Gate-on/off generally changes `G` little in low-memory settings (often around `±.03`), **but** effects can be larger in the no-proof, full-memory regime (report notes up to `.08`), so do not claim globally that the gate never matters.

**Default recommendation:** do **not** run further `q_c` values. Keep `q_c=12` and gate on for targeted experiments. Reopen this decision only if existing raw data reveal a material `q_c` dependence exactly in the regime being studied or if a proposed mechanism explicitly predicts such dependence.

### 5.4 Other things to avoid now

- No repeat of broad rate-by-budget or rate-by-window phase diagrams.
- No new large `eta`/`I` survey: useful supporting diagnostics, not core explanations.
- No immediate `N` scaling sweep. It becomes worthwhile **only after** an operational crossover has been mapped and a reduced model makes a falsifiable finite-size prediction.
- No new controller strategy, additional task worlds, or LLM-based agents in the first pass. First extract the mechanism from the already controlled finite-state/exact-Bayesian simulator.

## 7. New experiment campaign B (minimal): measure and perturb collective persistence

**Question:** Does a controller-induced deviation outlive passive memory because of ongoing communication, circulating controller-origin facts, or a self-sustaining bias in which agent-original facts are reposted?

**Step B0 — mandatory reuse:**

1. Check whether the full stored time series extend past `t=40`; whether trajectories, memory states, board contents, post provenance, `t_*` and actual post counts exist; and whether high-budget controller runs exhausted their budgets far before `t=30` (providing longer *effective* post-intervention intervals than the plots emphasize).
2. Re-estimate post-stop decay and uncertainty from episode-level controlled/silent differences, especially for `W=24`, `rho=.25` and `.5`. Do not claim an exponential lifetime if a plateau is unresolved.
3. If existing data already distinguish continued circulation of controller facts from state reinforcement, document it and reduce the new experiment accordingly.

**Only if B0 cannot resolve the mechanism, run ONE focused long-time continuation with controlled branches:**

- Choose one **high-persistence/short-memory** setting, preferably `rho=.25, q=6, W=24, lambda_c=1, q_c=12, B=30, gate on`, **in both task worlds**. Optionally add a single higher-memory contrast (`rho=.75`) *only if necessary* to demonstrate a different relaxation regime.
- Continue at least to `t=200` (extend selectively if there is no observable relaxation), with the same controller shutoff at `t=30`. Record actual last successful post `t_*` and provenance-tagged board/memory/agent-post histories.
- At `t=30` branch **the same realized states and future stochastic streams** into:
  **(i) normal continuation** (agents keep reading/voting/posting);  
  **(ii) communication interrupted** (agents continue activation, forgetting, Bayesian updating and voting, but cannot read or post new messages);  
  **(iii) controller-exclusive-fact purge** (remove facts exclusive to the controller's pool from currently readable posts and agent memories, then resume ordinary communication; retain facts independently available to agents).  
  If implementation or resources demand further reduction, make **(i) vs (ii)** indispensable and treat **(iii)** as an optional mechanistic refinement rather than a separate campaign.
- Critically, run **silent-arm matched branches under the same post-stop manipulation**. Stopping communication or purging facts changes the dynamics of the silent population too; always subtract the appropriate silent baseline.
- For proof-task pools, some facts overlap agents' initial facts: **do not indiscriminately delete all matching fact IDs** and thereby remove genuinely independent agent evidence. Distinguish controller-exclusive *semantic facts* from origin of a *particular message copy*, and state precisely which is purged. In no-proof, all pool facts are new, which is a cleaner provenance test.
- Record `x_A2`, paired `G(t)`, posterior means/entropy, fact identities and ages, frequency of controller-exclusive facts on board/in memories, and reproduction/recirculation of agent-origin facts.

**Interpretation:**

- Bias persists under normal continuation but rapidly decays without communication -> evidence that ongoing interaction sustains it, beyond simple passive individual memory.
- Bias collapses after controller-exclusive fact purge -> continued availability of the controller's distinctive evidence is important.
- Bias persists after purge but depends on communication -> consistent with self-reinforcing propagation of **agent-origin** evidence or other network-state memory, though verify mechanisms from logs.
- Bias persists even when communication is stopped -> inspect whether votes, memories, priors, or absorbing states make that possible; do **not** automatically call it an emergent communication-based memory.
- If no branch decays by the horizon, report **censored relaxation times / bounds**, do not force a finite `tau_collective`.

**Required output:** response-versus-time plots (normal and ablated, both tasks, with confidence intervals), factual provenance/turnover plots, estimates or right-censored bounds on persistence time relative to unreinforced microscopic times, and a reasoned account of which physical carrier (memory, board content, communication feedback) sustains the effect.

## 8. Secondary observation and possible future paper: intervention backfire

No-proof full memory shows a notable **negative marginal effect of more/faster false-targeted intervention**, while with shorter memory this backfire vanishes. The no-proof larger-budget runs amplify the contrast; extra messages can be useless or harmful despite all posted facts being true and agents being exact Bayesians. Proof-bearing high-memory runs are resistant for a different reason: conclusive evidence can accumulate, making the truth certain.

**Next action now:** Reanalyze the **existing** data for `G` against (a) messages sent, (b) *distinct* controller facts exposed, and (c) posterior approach to the full-evidence posterior `(0.75,0,.25)` in no-proof. Do not start another rate-budget sweep. If a general, predictive relationship emerges, revisit a distinct research question/paper on **optimal intervention intensity and informational backfire**. It is **not currently a third primary experimental campaign**.

## 9. Publication strategy and interpretation discipline

**Default: one integrated paper**, because the no-proof task supplies an essential contrast for both main phenomena:

- The reading reversal occurs **even without a proof**; proof-based reconstruction alone is too narrow an explanation.
- Persistent susceptibility occurs **even without a proof**; losing a distributed proof is not a necessary condition for collective memory effects.
- Differences in saturation/backfire show the role of the available evidence landscape, but are not yet an independent general finding.

A candidate guiding question is: **How do memory, communication, and the structure of available evidence determine whether interacting Bayesian agents integrate information accurately or maintain a self-reinforcing informational bias?**

Avoid overstatement:

- No established phase transition or universal exact critical value `rho=.5`: current `N=24`, sparse `rho/q` grids and single worlds establish at most operational crossovers.
- No established numerical `tau_collective` from the reported ten-unit post-stop window: measured persistence of an *effect* is not automatically a single intrinsic relaxation timescale.
- No confirmed mechanism of controller-fact recirculation: current aggregate plots do not track provenance.
- No causal proof-vs-no-proof attribution from the existing two worlds alone because task materials and controller evidence differ.
- The controller transmits **true, selectively chosen facts**, not fabricated false facts. Be precise: the study is about steering belief under evidence selection and limited memory, not necessarily false statements.
- Random probability matching makes individual votes stochastic even when evidence favors one answer; interpret population voting in terms of beliefs, sampling, and recurrent communication rather than individual irrationality.
