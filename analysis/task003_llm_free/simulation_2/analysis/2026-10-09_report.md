## Q1 — Simulation 1 against the core

`q1_silent.csv`, `q1_gains.csv`, figures `F4a`, `F4b`.

- **With full memory (ρ = 1) the core behaves like Simulation 1.** Gains differ by at most
  0.06 and on average by less than 0.01 (nosolution: 0.368 vs 0.377 target A0; 0.175 vs
  0.180 target A2). Symmetric reaches a proof of A0 in every agent in both, so all gains are 0.
- **With forgetting (ρ = 0.75) the core weakens control and the swarm's own consensus.**
  Mean over q and qc, core minus Simulation 1: nosolution −0.12 (target A0), −0.09 (target
  A2); symmetric −0.08 (target A2); symmetric target A0 unchanged (+0.005 at the main cell).
  Silent symmetric m_A0(30): 0.60 / 0.72 / 0.78 in the core against 0.65 / 0.84 / 0.94 in
  Simulation 1, for q = 3 / 6 / 12. Silent nosolution leans less toward A2 (m_A2 0.48 vs 0.53, q 6).
- Interpretation (not tested separately): in Simulation 1 an agent's memory changes only at
  dawn and it acts right after reading a full day's posts; in the core, facts expire at
  random times between actions and the agent's next action may come late, so fewer facts
  are held together at any moment, and joint evidence (proofs, strong combinations) is
  rarer. With ρ = 1 nothing expires and the difference vanishes.

## Q3 — The controller rate

`rate_steps.csv`, figures `F2_*`, `F3`.

- **ρ = 0.75: the rate matters little for G(30).** Main cells, gate on, rate 0.5 → 8:
  nosolution target A0 0.28 → 0.30, target A2 0.20 → 0.17; symmetric target A0 0.07 → 0.11,
  target A2 0.10 → 0.14. What changes is speed and cost: at rate 8 with the gate off the budget is spent by
  t ≈ 3.7 (median), gains peak early and **overshoot** (symmetric target A2: peak 0.26 at t ≈ 4.5,
  then 0.14 at t = 30), and messages per unit of gain rise. Efficiency (24·G(30)/messages)
  falls from 0.97 to 0.25 (nosolution target A0, gate on).
- **ρ = 1, nosolution: a higher rate gives a lower gain** (exploratory, §Q3-confirmation).
  Target A2, gate on, q 6, qc 12: 0.20 at rate 0.5, 0.14 at 1, 0.09 at 2, 0.06 at 4, 0.05 at
  8. 146 of 768 rate steps are significant reversals; the 18 of size ≥ 1/24 are all
  nosolution target A2, ρ = 1, rates 0.5 → 1 or 1 → 2, at every q and at qc 3–12, with both
  gate settings, so they meet the neighbouring-cell criterion.
- **Mechanism (exploratory):** with full memory every posted fact stays in the swarm. The
  agents' facts plus the **whole** pool give (0.75, 0, 0.25), so the more of the pool gets
  out, the closer beliefs move to that point, whatever the target. Across nosolution cells
  at ρ = 1, G(30) falls with the number of distinct pool facts posted per episode
  (slope −0.031 per fact for target A2, −0.016 for target A0; correlations −0.57, −0.47;
  `report2_distinct_facts.csv`). Rate drives that number: spread over 30 time units the
  controller reads varied boards and posts more distinct facts (6.3 at rate 1, gate off);
  at rate 8 it spends the budget in a burst on similar boards and repeats itself (5.0),
  which is why the gain partly recovers at rate 8. At ρ = 0.75 forgotten facts leave, and
  the relation is weak (slope −0.009 and 0.000). The same composition argument (true facts
  that do not favour the target) underlies Simulation 1's large-b backfire, through a
  different route (all-or-nothing posting there; accumulation of distinct facts here).

### Q3 confirmation (seed 2027, nosolution, ρ = 1)

Run `2026-10-09_sim2_rho1_confirm_seed2027` (nosolution, ρ 1, q 3/6/12, qc 6/12, every rate,
gate on/off, seed 2027; `rho1confirm_*.csv`). **The reversal is confirmed**: all 16 size ≥ 1/24
reversals of the main run that fall in these cells reappear with intervals below 0; 15 of 16
again have size ≥ 1/24 (the other −0.039). Step sizes correlate at 0.99 between the two runs.
Main cell, target A2, gate on: 0.198 / 0.150 / 0.084 / 0.054 / 0.053 for rates 0.5 … 8.
The explanation (distinct pool facts) rests on a correlation across settings and stays
**exploratory**; a direct test is a controller with memory (TODO).

## Q4 — Persistence

`gains.csv` (`persistence`), figure `F3`.

- G(40) − G(30) lies between −0.056 and +0.019 in every cell; 0 of 1,920 settings show a
  loss of 1/24 or more except 16 nosolution target A2 cells at ρ = 1 (down to −0.056).
  Main cells: within ±0.016. Confirmed on seed 2027 (H1).
- Ten time units without the controller leave its effect in place: the controller's facts
  have spread into the agents' posts (agents relay controller facts in 12–45% of their
  posts, `q8_mechanisms.csv`) and keep circulating.

## Q5 — The vote gate

`gate_effect.csv`, figure `F5`.

- **Gate off rarely helps.** It adds messages everywhere the gate used to stop the
  controller (up to +28 per episode) and raises the gain by ≥ 1/24 only for symmetric
  target A0 at ρ = 0.75 (12 of 60 settings; mean +0.025; H6 at the main cell +0.055).
- **On nosolution at ρ = 1 it lowers the gain** (target A0 mean −0.051, as low as −0.118;
  target A2 −0.027): more messages, more distinct facts, the mechanism of Q3.
- The false controller on symmetric is hardly ever stopped by the gate (the A2 share
  rarely reaches 75%), so gate off changes little there (+0.3 messages).

## Q6 — The proof stop

`stop_effect.csv`. Symmetric target A0 only. No setting shows an effect of 1/24 (largest
|effect| 0.019); with ρ = 0.75 24 of 60 (gate off) and 14 of 60 (gate on) show a small
one. Turning the stop off costs up to +24.6 messages per episode for nothing.

## Q7 — Sensing

`q7_sensing.csv`, figure `F6`.

- **v̂ overestimates the target's current share**, by +0.025 (gate on) to +0.055 (gate off)
  on average, up to +0.18, and **the bias grows with the rate**: about +0.014 at rates ≤ 1,
  +0.05 (gate on) and +0.14 to +0.15 (gate off) at rate 8 (mean over the grid). In Simulation 1 v̂ was unbiased. The mean absolute error falls
  with qc (0.16 at qc 3 to 0.07 at qc 24).
- Likely reason (not tested): the posts the controller reads come from agents who acted
  recently, and these have just read the controller's facts; the "true current share"
  counts every agent's last vote, including old ones. With the gate off the controller
  posts more, and the gap grows.

## Q8 — Mechanisms (exploratory, main cells)

`q8_mechanisms.csv`.

- **Help with the truth replaces the agents' own proofs** (symmetric, as in Simulation 1):
  silent, 12% of agents prove A0 at t = 30, all from their own facts. With the truth
  controller 20–30% prove A0, but from their own facts only 6% (rate 0.5, gate on) down to
  0–1% (gate off or high rates).
- The false controller also raises proofs of A0 (to 14–16%): its true facts complete
  agents' proofs; own-evidence proofs fall to 1–3%.
- Controller posts are 0.2–4% of all posts during control, but 12–45% of agents' posts
  relay a controller-only fact; per action agents read 0.26–0.41 new and 1.45–1.63 reactivated facts.

## What this changes for the next steps

1. The rate scan's main lesson: under a fixed budget, **rate matters through timing and
   diversity of what is said, not through the amount**; cheapest control is slow and gated.
2. The ρ = 1 reversal suggests testing a controller with memory (avoid repeating or
   widening what it has said) — a design change, not tested here.
3. The sensing bias is new to asynchrony and deserves a direct test (votes by time since
   the agent's last action).
