## 2. The balanced pool — how it works

Built offline by `scripts/local/build_balanced_controller_pool.py`, frozen to
`results/studies/musr_truthful_selective_task_calibration_01/tasks/task_004/controller/balanced_fact_pool.json`.

**Lean.** Each true fact is labelled by the allocation it most favours *when
read alone*, using the exact 14,388-world posterior. That label is its *lean*;
the size of the probability increase is its *strength*.

**The problem it fixes.** `task_004`'s non-decisive facts lean **25 / 9 / 9**
toward truth / A1 / A2. A controller handed that pool starts with a menu
slanted toward the answer it may be trying to hide.

**The construction.** Keep *all* 18 rival-leaning facts, then add 9
truth-leaning ones — giving **27 facts, 9 per allocation**. The 9 are chosen so
their *strength distribution* most closely matches the rivals', by minimum
**Wasserstein-1 distance** (the least total strength you would have to move to
turn one distribution into the other). Not the strongest 9, which would put the
slant straight back; not random, which would not be reproducible.

Achieved match: mean strength 0.0953 (truth) against 0.0921 (rivals),
Wasserstein-1 distance 0.0082.

**One pool, not two.** This is the design decision that matters most, and it
differs from what we had drafted. The controller's menu is **identical whichever
target it is given**. So any directional difference in outcome is attributable
to the controller's *selection policy*, not to its menu. Our draft
`symmetric_pools.json` (two disjoint 12-fact pools) cannot make that claim: two
different menus confound content with policy. Darius's design is strictly better
for the control-efficiency question.

**Bookkeeping.** The pool is a controller-only field and is excluded from the
paired-initialization hash, so existing `task_004` no-control baselines still
pair against the new controlled arms. That is what makes the baseline reusable
instead of needing a rerun per condition — exactly what v0.4 §1 asks for.

### The asymmetry that cannot be removed

The pool's joint posteriors:

| conditioning on | P(A0 truth) | P(A1) | P(A2) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| nothing (prior) | 0.333 | 0.333 | 0.333 |
| the 9 truth-leaning facts | 0.814 | 0.045 | 0.141 |
| the 9 A1-leaning facts | 0.277 | 0.675 | 0.048 |
| the 9 A2-leaning facts | 0.067 | 0.000 | **0.933** |
| **the whole 27-fact pool** | **1.000** | 0.000 | 0.000 |

Read the last two rows together. The A2 half is *genuinely persuasive* — on its
own it makes a wrong answer the favourite at 0.933 — but it is **not a proof**.
The pool as a whole **does** prove the truth.

So the controller can prove A0 and can only argue for A2. **This is not a bug
and cannot be designed away.** Two independent analyses reached it:

- Darius's design audit: *"All facts are true statements about one world, so any
  large enough set of them is jointly consistent only with worlds near the true
  one, and the pooled posterior drifts to truth no matter how individual lean is
  balanced. Count balance is the wrong knob."*
- `task_003_redesign.md`: *"A truthful false-target controller cannot have a
  logically sufficient proof of its false target while the actual world remains
  possible. Do not try to match truth/false proof availability to certainty."*

**Consequence for us.** Balance has to be defined at the *corpus* level — the
pooled posterior, or the strength of what each half proves alone — not by
counting facts. And the truth/false comparison must be reported as *correction
versus selective persuasion under the world's inherent asymmetry*, not as a
symmetric steering experiment. That sentence belongs in the paper.

## 3. The two controllers on offer

Both use the balanced pool. They differ in who decides what to post and when.

| | **A — LLM policy** | **B — classical / deterministic** |
|---|---|---|
| study dir | `29-09-2026-task004-balanced-b90-report-only-rho075` | `29-09-2026-task004-classical-b3-gated-balanced` |
| `controller_authoring` | `llm_authored` | `deterministic` |
| `advocacy_schedule` | `always` (gate off) | `soft` (gate on) |
| budget | `intervention_budget: 90`, scope `episode` | `intervention_budget: 3`, scope `per_round` |
| extras | — | `controller_round_budget_mode: exact` |
| v0.4 row | Priority **1** (full-episode budget) | Priority **2** (per-round budget) |

Shared by both: `target` A0 or A2, `sensing_mode: board`,
`sensor_sample_size: 100`, `policy: soft_target`, `threshold: 0.5`, `beta: 4.0`,
`controller_timing: dawn_only`, `controller_memory_mode: none`,
`controller_report_selection_strategy: target_preserving_v1`,
`controller_fact_pool_mode: balanced`.

**A** is the interesting object and the harder one: with an episode budget the
controller must decide *when* to spend, so temporal allocation becomes part of
the policy. v0.4 says as much.

**B** is the reference. `deterministic` authoring means the posting sequence is
a known function of the sensed state rather than something a model improvises.

## 5. Other things on that branch worth knowing

**`docs/design/musr_game_design_vs_implementation.md`** — compares our generator
with upstream MuSR. Two findings we should not rediscover:

- Our game keeps MuSR's *rules* but replaces its *evidence model*: 49 symbolic
  threshold/comparison propositions instead of MuSR's 9 exact-value facts, and
  canonical sentences instead of a narrative. A deliberate departure, and the
  paper should say **"MuSR-style"**, not "MuSR".
- Upstream has a real inconsistency: the paper says the gold allocation wins by
  at least 2, the code *shrinks* the margin to at most 2, and **15 of the 250
  released instances (6%) are exact ties** with no unique answer. Our generator
  enforces a unique winner, so we are stricter.

**`docs/design/swarm_scheduling_game_design.md`** — a proposed second testbed.
Out of scope now; relevant if a reviewer asks for a task beyond MuSR.

**Analysis tooling** in `scripts/local/`: `plot_round_trajectories.py`,
`plot_posted_facts.py`, `plot_fact_heatmap.py`, plus Phoenix trace
import/export. Check these before writing new plotting code.

## 6. Where `task_003_redesign.md` and `audit_target_pools.py` fit

Both sit in `../10_task_and_facts/`, written 1 October in a separate session,
preserved unchanged. They are the third independent look at the pool problem and
they agree with the other two.

`task_003_redesign.md` adds three things our own analysis did not have:

1. **A better proof test.** Whole-pool posterior, not proof counting. The
   historical pool gives whole-pool P(A0) = 0.308 < 1, which certifies it
   contains **no truth proof of any size**. Our "0 of 7,694 minimal proofs" was
   exhaustive only to size 6 — a weaker statement.
2. **Positive-lift pools overlap.** 15 facts raise *both* A0 and A2, by
   weakening A1. So target-specific construction does **not** give disjoint
   pools, and switching target is not flipping a sign. If we mean "evidence for
   A2 *over* A0", we must say so with a contrastive criterion:
   ΔP(A2) > 0 **and** P(A2) > P(A0). That pool is smaller (15 facts) and
   *collectively more persuasive* than the full 27 — adding true facts can
   lower P(false).
3. **Caveats on our numbers.** The 4 disjoint proofs are a packing *lower
   bound*; disjoint fact IDs do not mean statistically independent evidence; and
   the archived CSV does not record which agent held which fact, so the original
   assignment artifact must be kept for real experiments.

`audit_target_pools.py` is its evidence: it rebuilds `task_003` from
`V = (3,1,1,2,2,1,1,1,2)`, re-derives every fact's lift against the symbolic
engine, and compares eight candidate pools, writing
`task_003_pool_comparison.json`. Its hashes are over *sorted* membership, so
they are not comparable to the ordering hashes in existing studies.

**Two notes.** Its hardcoded input path still says `results/game_analysis`,
which no longer exists — it needs repointing to `../10_task_and_facts` before it
will run. And the document describes a code fix ("reject a frozen pool whenever
its declared target differs from the resolved target") that is **not present on
`dev/rsanchez`**: the permissive test
`test_selective_false_pool_can_be_reused_for_exact_truth_counterpart` is still
in `tests/mas_cc/test_selective_truth_controller.py`, and it is on
`darius-MA-v1` too. Either the fix lives somewhere we have not found, or it was
described as done without landing. Worth resolving before anyone reruns an old
truth-control config.
