## Summary

- **E2 failed because of my design.** At a fixed strength per fact, more facts also means more total evidence, so G
  rose monotonically to F_C = 16. Efficiency must be judged at **fixed total evidence**, F_C β_C = constant.
- **At fixed total evidence, the finite model has an optimum F_C\* that grows with q τ** (rank correlation 0.75,
  p = 0.001): 1 fact at q τ ≲ 8, 4 facts at q τ ≈ 16, 8 facts at q τ ≳ 30. The mean field shows no clear optimum, so
  the trade-off is a finite-size effect, as you predicted for copy-number questions.
- **Persistence:** concentration keeps the controller's *facts* alive after the stop (87% against 30% surviving at
  q = 4, ρ = 0.135, for F_C = 1 against 16), but the retained *effect* barely depends on F_C.
- **E4 met:** doubling the controller's rate raises G only 1.2–1.3×.
- **E1 met**, **E3 mean-field part failed**, **E5 mean-field part failed** (nearly flat).
- **Data:** the real controller uses 4–8 distinct facts per episode out of 10–12, and spreads more at long memory.
