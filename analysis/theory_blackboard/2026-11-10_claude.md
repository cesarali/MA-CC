## 2. New: first LLM results (Design R, collaborator's run, 7 Nov)

These are in swarm-control, `docs/research-site/pages/RES-05-v9/` and `RES-10-v3/`. They use the symmetric task,
15 GPT-OSS-120B agents, an LLM controller, q ∈ {3, 6, 12}, 8 retentions, T = 30, horizon 40 and 100 paths per cell.
- **LLM gains are large** (up to 58 pp) and persist to t = 40.
- **Long memory:** reading more hurts, as in the Bayesian run. Δ_q = G(q12) − G(q3) is −11 to −17 pp at ρ = 0.5–0.875,
  against −6 for Bayesian R.
- **Short memory:** the positive branch is missing. Δ_q is about 0 (−5.5 to +0.7) at ρ ≤ 0.375, against +12 to +24 for
  Bayesian R.

So the reversal is not absent: its short-memory half is missing.

I checked whether the LLM run is faithful to our Bayesian simulator:
- **The event kernel is a port of our Simulation 2,** at MA-CC commit c93dec4. Reading, renewal on reading, exponential
  forgetting at γ = −ln ρ, the own-post exclusion and the window all match.
- **The facts differ from our 15-agent setups.** The agents' facts and the A2 pool are different sets (8 of 15 and 8 of
  12 facts differ). Their properties are similar: the agents prove A0; the pool alone gives (0.10, 0, 0.90); agents
  plus pool prove A0. Other settings also differ: W = 48 (ours 30), q_c = 12 (ours 8), horizon 40 (ours 130).
- **Four differences in the decision policy, not in the environment:**
  1. **Vote:** "Vote for the allocation most likely to be best". This is an argmax instruction, not probability
     matching.
  2. **The agent's previous vote is shown** in every prompt, a possible source of inertia (their RES-06 assessment).
  3. **Posting:** "the fact that would most help the group", or NONE. This is not our support rule, under which the
     agent posts a fact that supports its vote.
  4. **The controller is an LLM** choosing among pool facts, with no memory, instead of target-posterior scoring.

  The run changes all four at once, so nothing identifies which one removes the short-memory branch.

My hypothesis for you to judge: **the argmax vote is the prime suspect.** At short memory, agents hold one or two
facts. A thresholded response ignores small evidence changes until one answer leads, so extra reading cannot raise the
gain smoothly. Under probability matching it can. This is testable in our Bayesian simulator without any LLM, by
switching the vote rule to argmax, and also with the posting and vote-feedback rules.
