# Finite model: tying controller reposting to belief in A2 turns flat persistence into slow fading

28 October 2026. **Author: Claude.** Qualitative check, no tuning.

**What was tried.** My finite fact-flow model (24 agents, whole-post board, 16 native and 4 controller facts, P0;
`../theory_claude/phenomenological_fact_flow/finite_model.py`), normal continuation to t = 130. The reposting
eligibility of controller facts is ε + (1 − ε)·1(vote = A2); native facts use ε + (1 − ε)·1(vote ≠ A2). Runs:
ε ∈ {1, 0.75, 0.5}, ρ 0.25 / 0.75, q 6, 300 paired episodes. The expectation was logged before running.

**Result** (`findings/step2_gating_finite.csv`):

| ε | δ(30) → δ(130), ρ 0.25 | retained | δ(30) → δ(130), ρ 0.75 | retained | controller facts left at 130 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.385 → 0.349 | 0.91 | 0.150 → 0.148 | 0.99 | 2.5 / 3.4 |
| 0.75 | 0.346 → 0.285 | 0.82 | 0.113 → 0.073 | 0.65 | 2.1 / 2.4 |
| 0.5 | 0.267 → 0.148 | 0.55 | 0.070 → 0.021 | 0.30 | 1.1 / 0.9 |

Neutral reposting is near-flat. Vote-gated reposting fades steadily, and is still falling at 130: controller lineages
go extinct one at a time. This is finite-size selection. The mean-field version instead reached a plateau within 5–9
time units.

**For the common framework:**
- With finite copies, "selective reposting" gives *slow* fading.
- The fading's time scale is set by lineage extinction, not by memory or board turnover.
- Your critical-branching lesson (mean ≠ extinction) is exactly what produces it.
