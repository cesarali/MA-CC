# New question from the researcher: how efficiently can a controller use a given number of facts?

8 November 2026. **Author: Claude.** Researcher's request: work on this together. You take the analytic side, I do

and my metastability report, is at the end.

## 1. The researcher's idea (please judge it)

The fact-flow theory is about facts, and both phenomena are tied to which facts circulate. The researcher proposes to
measure controller **efficiency in terms of facts**: how much effect a controller gets from a given number of distinct
facts F_C, and from a given posting budget.

My first reading of the theory. At a controller board share b_C, Eq. 1 gives
M_C = F_C r_C / (r_C + γ), with r_C = 1 − (1 − b_C / F_C)^q.
- **Mean field:** M_C increases with F_C toward q b_C τ. With few facts, reads hit facts already held (wasted
  redundancy), and M_C ≤ F_C caps the evidence an agent can carry. So spreading is better.
- **Finite swarm:** at a fixed budget, more facts means fewer copies per fact, hence faster extinction (your exact
  lineage results). So concentration is better.
- **Hence an interior optimum F_C\*,** expected to grow with q τ. Wasted repeat reads dominate when agents read a lot and
  remember long; extinction dominates otherwise.
- **Persistence:** the mean-field threshold R_C = q κ_C g_C / (γ H) does not depend on F_C. Only finite size can make
  concentrated facts outlive the stop.

Please judge whether this is the right framing, and whether "efficiency" should be defined per post, per distinct
fact, or per unit of total evidence (F_C β_C).

## 3. My part: pre-registration (written before any computation)

**Models:**
- mean-field `fact_flow.py` and finite `finite_model.py`;
- P0 except N = 15, W = 30 and F_A = 15, to match the 15-agent campaign; ε = 1; T = 30; continuation to t = 130.

**Scan:**
- F_C ∈ {1, 2, 4, 8, 16};
- controller rate λ0 ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}, i.e. budget ≈ 15, 30, 60 posts;
- q ∈ {2, 4, 8} (finite) and {1, 2, 4, 8, 16} (mean field);
- ρ ∈ {0.135, 0.37, 0.61, 0.78, 0.88};
- finite: 400 paired episodes per cell.

**Total-evidence control:** β_C = 2/F_C (so F_C β_C = 2, the P0 total), at λ0 = 1.

**Predictions:**
- **E1 (mean field, spreading wins):** at fixed λ0 and β_C, g(30) increases with F_C at every (q, ρ).
- **E2 (finite, an interior optimum):** at λ0 = 1, G(30) peaks at an interior F_C\* ∈ {2, 4, 8} in at least half of
  the cells. F_C\* correlates positively with q τ (Spearman > 0).
- **E3 (concentration buys persistence):**
  - mean field: retained g(130)/g(30) varies by less than 0.1 across F_C;
  - finite: retained G(130)/G(30) decreases with F_C in at least 70% of the cells where G(30) > 2 se.
- **E4 (diminishing returns):** G(30) at λ0 = 2 is less than twice G(30) at λ0 = 1, in every cell, in both models.
- **E5 (fixed total evidence):** with F_C β_C = 2, the mean field favours concentration (g(30) decreasing in F_C),
  because a smaller board share per fact lowers m_C. The finite model favours it even more. Low confidence for the
  finite part.
- **D1 (data check, campaign posts):** per episode, the simulator's controller posts on average at most 6 distinct
  facts. The number of distinct controller facts circulating at t = 30 lies within a factor 2 of the finite model's
  F_C\* at the matching (q, ρ). Low confidence; the real controller's choice is evidence-driven, not a fixed F_C.

## 4. Secondary point: the "Indirect tipping" paper and my metastability report

The researcher asks you to read both carefully and propose ideas:
- **the paper:** Flint, Aiello, Constantino, Pastor-Satorras, Baronchelli, "Indirect tipping: a social attack surface
  in AI agent populations", arXiv:2609.25194 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.25194);
- **my report:** `../15_agent_simulation/reports/2026-11-03_metastability/metastability_report.pdf`, with tables and
  scripts beside it.

My summary of the connection:
- **Different mechanism.** The paper's metastable states are conventions held up by a payoff for agreeing. Ours are
  information states: which facts survive, given that extinction is permanent and that support-based reposting
  recirculates the majority's facts.
- **Analogues in the data:**
  - a phase diagram of end states (mixed, A2 lock-in, A0 lock-in) with the A2 band on the q τ diagonal;
  - bistability under attack at the edge of that band (task003 symmetric, q = 8, ρ = 0.61: 40% of episodes end at
    A2 ≥ 0.8, 35% at A2 ≤ 0.2);
  - durable tipping (86–90% of A2 lock-ins survive 100 time units after the stop);
  - the paper's "unintended diversion": in no-solution at long memory the false controller raises A0 lock-in by up to
    0.70.

**Questions for you:**
- Where does the mapping hold, and where does it break?
- Is our "information state" a defensible notion of metastability? What would make it rigorous, for example dwell
  times, or a quasi-potential from the fact-flow model?
- Which of the paper's tools (critical mass, asymmetric landscape, stepping stones, timing) would be most informative
  for us, and how would you set them up in our framework?
