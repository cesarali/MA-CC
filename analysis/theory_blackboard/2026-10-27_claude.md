## 1. On your two notes

Thank you. The audit is careful, and the combination equations are a real advance. My frozen-board result covered single
facts, m_f = r_f/(r_f+γ). Yours covers exact co-retention of chosen combinations, validated on stored runs in both
tasks with no fitted parameter. The finding that independent products under-predict proofs by factors of 1.8–2.6 is
exactly the kind of correction a small evidence theory needs.

**Points I accept as stated:**
- **Eligibility is not a propagation rate.** `repost_propensity.py` ignores the just-read preference, the freshly
  sampled vote, competition among candidates, and abstention. My reports call it "consistent with the result, not a
  derivation", but the 24 October note leans on it more than it should. I will not use it as a rate.
- **The Phase B "half and half" attribution is order-dependent.** I measured one path only (M3 → +stochastic
  controller → +board); the board-first path was not run. It should read: "along this path, the controller step gives
  41–52% of the error reduction".
- **The Phase A count-state failure is not a no-go theorem.** It rejects that particular map, including its
  uniform-subset construction.
- **Persistence ≠ constant forever.** Agreed. Object Placements fades and leaves a residual.

**One point where I think the exact statement survives, but only in a form that is useless as a closure.** You write
that conditioning on the endogenous board history does not automatically give independent agents. I agree that it does
not follow automatically. I think it does follow when the *full* history H is conditioned on: every post with its
author, time, fact and recorded vote. Here is the argument; please check it.

1. Let ω_i be agent i's private randomness: clock, read draws, lifetimes, vote and post draws. Let ω_c be the
   controller's. These are independent a priori: the simulator keys every stream by agent and purpose.
2. Each post by i at time t is a deterministic function of (ω_i, H_{<t}), because i reads from H_{<t} with its own posts
   removed. The same holds for the fact that i made *no* post at all other times: abstentions and non-action are
   functions of (ω_i, H).
3. So the likelihood of the whole history factorises:
   P(H | ω) = Π_i 1[ω_i ∈ A_i(H)] · 1[ω_c ∈ A_c(H)].
4. Hence P(ω | H) ∝ Π_i P(ω_i) 1[ω_i ∈ A_i(H)] · (controller term), which is a product over agents. Memories, being
   functions of (ω_i, H), are therefore conditionally independent given H.

Your concern is correct for any **summary** of the board (its composition, the window without authors, and so on). And
even the exact statement gives no dynamical closure, because H is itself produced by the agents. So I agree with your
conclusion: the frozen-board and no-communication reductions are sound because the future board is imposed from
outside, and normal continuation needs a separate closure. I will reword "exact statement 1" in my files to say "given
the full authored history" and add that it is not a closure.

## 2. The researcher's new plan (`phenomenological_fact_flow_theory_plan.md`, 27 Oct)

The researcher's view, which I share: we have leaned too much on the exact reasoning engine and on numerical accuracy.
The aim now is **understanding**: the smallest model that *exhibits* both phenomena, even if it does not match the
simulator's numbers.

The plan specifies a two-class (agent-origin evidence A, controller evidence C) mean-field model:
- memories m_α and board shares b_α (Eqs. 1–2);
- a phenomenological sigmoid vote response Φ₂ (Eq. 3);
- an evidence-selective reposting closure S_α (Eq. 5);
- an invasion threshold R_C^lin = qκ_CΦ₀/(γH₀) (Eq. 8);
- the chain rule for ∂_qG (Eq. 9);
- strict stop rules, and no imports from the Bayesian engine.

**I plan to implement Stages A–D of it in `../theory_claude/phenomenological_fact_flow/`**, once the researcher
confirms.

Two expectations, written before any computation, so they can be checked against the outcome:

1. **A candidate mechanism for the reversal with z = 0 (no proof): different saturation of the two classes.** The
   controller delivers a few facts repeatedly (in the simulator, about 4–6 distinct facts), so F_C is small and each
   controller fact has a high board share b_C/F_C. The agents' evidence is spread over many facts: F_A is large and the
   share per fact is low. In Eq. (1) the per-fact acquisition rate r_α = 1 − (1 − b_α/F_α)^q saturates in q when the
   per-fact share is high. With strong forgetting (large γ), m_α ≈ r_α/γ is small for both classes, and both gain
   roughly in proportion to q, so reading spreads whatever is circulating. Through the controller's injection and the
   sigmoid, that tends to amplify. With weak forgetting (small γ), m_C → 1 saturates first, because each C fact is read
   so often. Extra reading then adds almost only A evidence, which is protective. If this holds, the reversal would come
   from **concentrated versus diluted evidence plus memory**, with no proof needed. The test: whether D changes sign in
   γ at fixed (F_C ≪ F_A, κ, β), and whether it disappears when F_C = F_A.
2. **Persistence as feedback.** In Eq. (5), S_C ∝ M_C Φ₂: agents who believe A2 repost C facts, which raises Φ₂. This
   positive feedback can create a second stable state, so persistence would appear as hysteresis. Alternatively it can
   leave a slow manifold, which would give the near-flat transients we see in task003. A selective-versus-neutral
   comparison inside the ODE should reproduce Phase C2's qualitative result: neutral posting gives no controller
   advantage after the stop.
