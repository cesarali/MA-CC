## A point I think we both missed

In Object Placements no-proof, controller **board share** is flat after the stop (0.68 → 0.67 and 0.43 → 0.46), while δ
falls 60–70%. My finite model's fading (vote-gated reposting) works by **lineage extinction**: the controller's board
share falls. So the finite model fades for a reason the simulator does not show, at least in this setup. Something
other than total exposure must fall. There are three engine-free candidates, and one diagnostic separates them.

1. **Controller diversity loss at constant share.** The board keeps the same share of controller posts, but on fewer
   distinct controller facts. Agents then hold fewer *distinct* controller facts (lower M_C), so there is less evidence.
   This is your _10 point about constant share against constant identities. It would be level 1 plus finite size, with
   no context variable.
2. **Operating point.** Strengths in log-odds stay fixed, but the receivers' log-odds x move, so σ′ changes. This is
   the sensitivity part of level 1.
3. **Contextual strength.** The log-odds strengths θ_f(M) of encountered facts themselves shrink or turn negative.
   Only this would require level 3.

## Step 1 (pre-registered here, before computing): the chain from exposure to vote

**Data:** stored normal-continuation runs (q = 6; ρ = 0.25 and 0.75; four setups; controlled arm `false` and silent
arm; t = 30, 40, 80, 130; the first 300 episodes per cell). These are the same runs as the mixture probe. The exact
posterior is used only as a **measuring instrument** on stored memories; the theory built afterwards stays engine-free.

**Measured, per cell and time:**
- (a) **Counts, engine-free:**
  - controller board share b_C (share of the 48 newest posts carrying a controller-exclusive fact);
  - the number of distinct controller-exclusive facts on the board, F_C^board;
  - the number of distinct controller-exclusive facts in the swarm (board ∪ memories), F_C^swarm;
  - per agent, the number of distinct controller-exclusive facts held, M_C;
  - per agent, native facts held in each arm, M_A^C and M_A^S.
- (b) **Operating point:** the mean and spread of x(M) = logit P(A2 | M) in the controlled arm (clipped to ±12).
- (c) **Log-odds strengths of encountered facts:** for each (agent, encountered fact f not held) pair, weighted by
  w_t(f), θ = logit P(A2 | M ∪ f) − logit P(A2 | M). Reported:
  - the share with θ > 0;
  - the mean θ among positive pairs;
  - the mean |θ| among negative pairs;
  - the share of pairs where f is already held.
- (d) **Operating-point counterfactual:**
  - S_θ30(t) = E_{M∼μ_t} E_{θ∼ν_30}[σ(x(M) + θ) − σ(x(M))], where ν_30 is the cell's pooled strength distribution at
    t = 30;
  - compare S_θ30(130)/S(30) with the measured S(130)/S(30).
- (e) A, C, I and Z(t), as above, with conditional means labelled.

**Predictions:**
- **P1 (diversity).** In OP no-proof, F_C^board and M_C fall by more after the stop than in task003 (relative fall at
  t = 130 at least 1.5× task003's), while b_C stays within ±10%. Confidence: moderate.
- **P2 (strength, not operating point).** In OP no-proof, S_θ30(130)/S(30) stays above 0.8, so the operating point
  alone explains less than half of the S drop. Confidence: low-moderate.
- **P3 (magnitudes, not signs).** In OP no-proof, the share of positive θ changes by less than 0.1, while the mean
  positive θ falls by more than 25%. Confidence: low. This answers your question about migration into negative contexts
  against shrinking positive effects.

**What each outcome means:**
- **P1 met and P2/P3 failed:** fading is controller diversity loss at constant share (level 1 + finite size). The law
  in step 2 is a copying law among controller lineages, with no context variable.
- **P2 failed** (S_θ30 explains ≥ 75% of the drop): S is a derived quantity. Its fall is a move of operating point, and
  the question becomes what moves x̄_c.
- **Only P2 met:** the strengths themselves change, and level 3 is needed. The law in step 2 is then a two-class
  context law, with sign share and magnitude handled as P3 says.
