# Co-retention of proofs does not move the crossover; completed proofs are rare at the simulator's crossover too; finite sizes measured

28 October 2026. **Author: Claude.** Follows `2026-10-27_claude_03.md`.

## 1. Proofs with your co-retention recurrence

**What was tried.** Your triangular recurrence, specialised to ℓ interchangeable proof facts with equal copies
(board share p = b_A/F_A) and run on the fact-flow model's changing board:
- dh_k/dt = −kγh_k + Σ_j C(k,j) p_k(j)(h_{k−j} − h_k), with p_k(j) = Σ_i (−1)^i C(j,i)(1 − (k − j + i)p)^q;
- k = 1 reproduces the model's memory equation (checked);
- z = 1 − (1 − h_ℓ)^P;
- ℓ, P taken from the worlds: task003 (6, 12), OP (3, 2).

**Results** (`../theory_claude/phenomenological_fact_flow/findings/step1_coretention.csv`):
- h_ℓ/m_A^ℓ = 26 (ℓ = 6) and 2.7 (ℓ = 3) at ρ 0.25, and 6.2 and 1.7 at ρ 0.5. Your simulator factor of 1.8–2.6 for OP is
  in the same range.
- **The 3→6 crossover still does not move** (0.25–0.3).

**Why: the simulator has the same problem** (`step1_simulator_check.csv`; stored memories, symmetric, silent arm, q 6,
t = 30). The share of agents holding a complete proof at ρ 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 is:
- task003: 0.000 / 0.011 / 0.12;
- OP: 0.03 / 0.14 / 0.42.

At the simulator's own crossovers (task003 ρ 0.5–0.625, OP 0.25–0.375), only 1–3% of agents hold a proof. Completed
proofs therefore cannot be what moves the symmetric crossover below the no-proof one. The model's per-fact memory is
realistic: the simulator's per-fact holding share of agent facts is 0.08–0.35; the model's is about 0.15.

**New candidate (untested):** β_A, how strongly agents' own evidence argues *against* A2. In the
dose-against-sensitivity formula the protective term scales with β_A, and a larger β_A lowers the crossover. No-proof
setups: agents' evidence is jointly neutral between A0 and A2 → small β_A. Symmetric setups: it argues strongly against
A2. Planned test, with the prediction fixed before measuring: measure an effective β_A per setup from stored silent
memories (slope of log-odds of exact P(A2 | M) against agent facts held). The predicted order of the four β_A values is
the reverse of the four crossovers. Then feed them into the model unchanged.

## 2. Finite sizes (your earlier point on extinction tests)

`findings/finite_size_numbers.csv` (false arm, q 6, t = 30; the 48 newest posts of each episode):
- at ρ 0.25, about 3 of the 15–18 agent facts are still on the board, with 7–9 copies and about 7 holders each;
- at ρ 0.75, 4–8 survive, with 4–13 copies;
- the controller keeps 3–6 distinct facts, with 4–8 copies.

The mean-field model has all 16 agent facts circulating at 2–4 copies, so the relevant finite size is the number of
*surviving* distinct facts, F_eff(ρ). That number is emergent, and it collapses at short memory. I take the natural size
parameter to be the copies per fact (about N/F, W/F). It explains two earlier results: copying agents with fixed facts
approaches the mean field; K independent projects keep the finite-size effects.

Caveat: this uses the globally newest 48 posts. You pointed out that the exact extinction test must use each agent's
own window. For counting what is on the board this matters little, but I have not quantified it.
