\subsection{Empirical calibration of directional affinity and compliance}
\label{subsec:effective_affinity}

The single-affinity theory describes a controlled interaction using a directional
bias $h$ and a kinetic compliance $\gamma$. To connect these quantities to the
LLM dynamics, we use the microscopic transitions observed in the state-matched
intervention experiment. We coarse-grain each focal agent's vote into two
states: supporting the controller target $Z$, or not supporting $Z$. Among
controlled updates, define
\[
p_+=P(\text{non-}Z\rightarrow Z\mid\text{controlled}),
\qquad
p_-=P(Z\rightarrow\text{non-}Z\mid\text{controlled}).
\]
Under the finite-compliance kernel,
\[
p_+=\gamma\sigma(h),
\qquad
p_-=\gamma\sigma(-h).
\]
Therefore the two parameters are directly identifiable:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\gamma_{\rm eff}=p_++p_-,
\qquad
h_{\rm eff}=\ln\frac{p_+}{p_-}.}
\label{eq:empirical_single_affinity_calibration}
\end{equation}
The sum measures how readily a controlled opportunity produces any revision,
whereas the ratio measures the direction favored conditional on revision.

Pooling all controlled updates gives
\[
\boxed{h_{\rm eff}=3.83,\qquad \gamma_{\rm eff}=0.372,}
\]
with forward-to-reverse transition ratio $e^{h_{\rm eff}}\simeq46.2$. Thus
motion toward the controller target is strongly favored among revisions, while
most controlled opportunities still leave the focal vote unchanged. A
stratified episode-level bootstrap gives the approximate $95\%$ interval
\[
\boxed{h_{\rm eff}\in[3.06,4.88].}
\]

No further decomposition of $h_{\rm eff}$ is required by the finite-time
thermodynamic theory. The same measured affinity enters the susceptibility,
the controlled current, and the path identity
$\Delta S_{\rm sys}+hJ_c+I(n_k;Y_k)=\Sigma$.
