\section{Efficiency of Feedback Control}
\label{sec:feedback_efficiency}

A central distinction in our formulation is that feedback-control thermodynamics does not require the population to have reached equilibrium, or even a stationary nonequilibrium state. Stochastic thermodynamics can be formulated directly for finite-time trajectories, where irreversibility is quantified by comparing forward and time-reversed path probabilities and the corresponding entropy balance includes the change of the system distribution itself \cite{irisarri2026stochastic}. Stationary limits are often emphasized because they simplify the theory: once the distribution no longer changes in time, boundary terms vanish and quantities such as currents, work, and information flow can be expressed as steady rates. This is the regime used, for example, in the feedback-cooling analysis of Horowitz and Sandberg \cite{horowitz2014second}. Our setting is different: the LLM population is deliberately observed during a finite sequence of feedback rounds, while its collective state is still evolving.

For this reason, we treat control efficiency as a finite-time quantity. During one feedback cycle, the controller acquires information about the current population, decides whether to intervene, and applies a biased actuation that changes the population state. Part of the controller input produces useful target-directed current, while part is associated with irreversibility and with the fact that the population distribution itself changes between the beginning and end of the cycle. The corresponding system-entropy change, \(\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}\), therefore cannot be discarded as it would be in a stationary calculation; it is precisely the term that accounts for operating away from steady state. This allows us to define a bounded efficiency of feedback control without assuming that the multi-agent population has equilibrated or reached a nonequilibrium steady state.

We therefore isolate the feedback-control channel rather than assigning a
thermodynamics to the full language-model population. Let
\(n\in\{0,\ldots,N\}\) denote the number of agents currently supporting the
controller target \(Z\), and let
\[
x=\frac{n}{N}
\]
be the corresponding target fraction. Our aim is to determine how efficiently
finite sensing and actuation resources are converted into a directed response
over one population round.

\subsection{Finite-information feedback controller}

The controller observes a sample of \(q_c\) agents without replacement.  If
\(Y_k\) is the number of target supporters in the sample at round \(k\), then
\[
Y_k\mid n_k=n \sim \mathrm{Hypergeom}(N,n,q_c).
\]
We define the controller feedback policy by
\[
\pi(u\mid y)
\equiv P(U_k=u\mid Y_k=y),
\qquad
\pi(1\mid y)
=
\sigma\!\left[
\beta\left(\theta-\frac{y}{q_c}\right)
\right],
\qquad
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},
\]
where \(U_k=1\) denotes advocacy and \(U_k=0\) denotes no intervention.  Thus
\(U_k\) is drawn from \(\pi(\cdot\mid Y_k)\) after sensing.  The
state-dependent intervention probability is therefore
\[
a_n
=
P(U_k=1\mid n_k=n)
=
\sum_y
P(Y_k=y\mid n_k=n)\,\pi(1\mid y).
\]

When the controller acts, a controlled microscopic interaction has finite
directional bias \(h_c\). Writing
\[
p_h=\sigma(h_c),
\]
a controlled update favors the target state with probability \(p_h\), while
the reverse transition remains possible with probability \(1-p_h\). Thus
\(h_c\) is a dimensionless control affinity, or equivalently the log-odds
\[
h_c
=
\log\frac{p_h}{1-p_h}.
\]
The deterministic one-sided controller is recovered only in the singular
limit \(h_c\to\infty\).

\subsection{Control susceptibility and exact response}
\label{sec:control-susceptibility}

The intervention probability $a_n$ specifies whether the controller acts,
whereas $h_c$ specifies the directional bias when it does. Conditional on the
same initial state $x$, we quantify the resulting population response through
the state-dependent susceptibility
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\chi(x)
&\equiv
\mathbb{E}\!\left[x' \mid U=1,x\right]
-\mathbb{E}\!\left[x' \mid U=0,x\right] \\
&=
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta x\mid U=1,x\right]
-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Delta x\mid U=0,x\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\label{eq:control-susceptibility}
\end{equation}
Here $x'$ is the target fraction after the round and $\Delta x=x'-x$. Thus,
$\chi(x)>0$ means that advocacy increases target support relative to the
uncontrolled dynamics, whereas $\chi(x)<0$ indicates a net response away from
the target.

For the finite-bias $q=1$ controller, this susceptibility can be obtained
exactly. After $b$ controlled opportunities,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\chi_{h_c}(x)
=
\left[\sigma(h_c)-x\right]\Lambda_b,
\qquad
\Lambda_b
=
1-\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right)^b
}.
\label{eq:chi_finite_bias}
\end{equation}
The response separates into the directional term $\sigma(h_c)-x$ and the
actuation factor $\Lambda_b$, which determines how strongly the finite budget
is expressed at population level. It vanishes at
$x^\star=\sigma(h_c)$, the intrinsic set point of the finite-bias controller.

\subsection{Information--response efficiency}

The first efficiency is purely information-theoretic and does not require a
thermodynamic interpretation.  Let
\[
R_0(\cdot\mid n)
\quad\text{and}\quad
R_{h_c}(\cdot\mid n)
\]
denote the next-round target-count distributions under no intervention and
advocacy, respectively.  The state-local action-to-population information is
\begin{equation}
T_\pi(n)
\equiv
I(U_k;n_{k+1}\mid n_k=n)
=
\operatorname{JS}_{a_n}
\!\left[
R_0(\cdot\mid n),
R_{h_c}(\cdot\mid n)
\right].
\label{eq:action-population-information}
\end{equation}
The subscript \(\pi\) indicates that this information channel is induced by
the chosen feedback policy: \(\pi\), together with the sensor model, determines
the within-state action distribution \(a_n\).  This quantity is reported in
bits. Because the target fraction lies in \([0,1]\), total
variation together with Pinsker's inequality gives the exact bound
\begin{equation}
T_\pi(n)
\ge
\frac{2a_n(1-a_n)}{\ln 2}\,
\chi_{h_c}(n/N)^2.
\label{eq:information-response-bound}
\end{equation}
A complete derivation is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:information_response_bound}.
This motivates the bounded information--response efficiency
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\eta_{\mathrm{IR}}(n)
=
\frac{
2a_n(1-a_n)\chi_{h_c}(n/N)^2
}{
(\ln 2)\,T_\pi(n)
}
\le 1.
}
\label{eq:eta_ir}
\end{equation}
It measures how much of the action-dependent change in the full next-state
distribution is expressed as a coherent shift of its mean target fraction.
It is not an energetic efficiency.

\subsection{Thermodynamic efficiency of the feedback-control layer}

To obtain a genuine thermodynamic efficiency, the controlled interaction must
have finite forward and reverse transition probabilities.  We therefore
introduce an opposing dimensionless load \(h_e\), so that the net affinity of
the reversible actuation channel is
\[
F=h_c-h_e,
\qquad
p_F=\sigma(F).
\]
The controlled current \(J_c\) counts the expected net number of
controller-directed transitions. For a state distribution \(p(n)\),
\[
J_c
=
N\Lambda_b
\sum_{n=0}^{N}
p(n)a_n
\left[
\sigma(h_c-h_e)-\frac{n}{N}
\right].
\]
We associate generalized input and output work with the two affinities,
\[
W_{\mathrm{ctrl}}=h_cJ_c,
\qquad
W_{\mathrm{out}}=h_eJ_c.
\]

The feedback controller also acquires information through its sensor.  The
relevant information resource is therefore
\[
I_{\mathrm{sens}}
=
I(n_k;Y_k),
\]
which is distinct from the downstream action-to-population information
\(T_\pi(n)=I(U_k;n_{k+1}\mid n_k=n)\) used in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_ir}.  The former is sensing information acquired about the
current population state; the latter is information carried from the selected
action into the post-actuation population state.  For the reversible
actuation/load layer, comparison of the forward and reverse path measures
gives
\[
\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}
+
(h_c-h_e)J_c
+
I(n_k;Y_k)
=
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_F\Vert P_R)
\ge 0.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
h_eJ_c
\le
h_cJ_c
+
I(n_k;Y_k)
+
\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}.
\]
For positive useful current, the finite-time thermodynamic efficiency is
therefore
\[
\boxed{
\eta_{\mathrm{th}}
=
\frac{h_eJ_c}
{
h_cJ_c+I(n_k;Y_k)+\Delta S_{\mathrm{sys}}
}
=
\frac{h_eJ_c}
{
h_eJ_c+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_F\Vert P_R)
}
\le 1.
}
\label{eq:eta_th}
\]

\subsection{Main theoretical consequences}

The efficiency formulation leads to four conclusions that we use throughout
the empirical analysis.

First, \(\eta_{\mathrm{IR}}\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{th}}\) answer different
questions and involve different information measures.  The former quantifies
how efficiently a controller-dependent population transition is converted
into mean behavioral response, whereas the latter quantifies how efficiently
control work and acquired sensing information are converted into useful work
against an opposing load.

Second, stronger control is not necessarily more efficient. Increasing
\(h_c\) can increase the directed current while increasing the generalized
input work \(h_cJ_c\) even faster. In the deterministic limit
\(h_c\to\infty\), the original one-sided controller is recovered
behaviorally, but its affinity-based work diverges; finite bias is therefore
essential for a non-singular thermodynamic efficiency.

Third, more sensing is not automatically better utilized. Increasing
\(q_c\) can increase \(I(n_k;Y_k)\) without producing a proportional increase in
useful current, so additional information may reduce
\(\eta_{\mathrm{th}}\).

Finally, the thermodynamic statement is deliberately restricted to the
reversible feedback-control layer.  The uncontrolled social dynamics may
contain absorbing transitions and hidden epistemic memory; these do not
invalidate the control-layer bound, but they prevent us from identifying
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_th} with the complete microscopic thermodynamic efficiency
of the full multi-agent population.
