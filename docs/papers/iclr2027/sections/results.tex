\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We organize the results around three complementary questions. First, how much
can the controller move the population? Second, how efficiently is the
controller-dependent information expressed as coherent population motion?
Third, how efficiently is the finite-time thermodynamic control budget converted
into directed current? These questions correspond respectively to the
susceptibility $\chi$, the information--response efficiency $\eta_{\rm IR}$,
and the thermodynamic efficiency $\eta_{\rm th}$.

\subsection{Control susceptibility is causal, finite, and state dependent}

We first measure the response to advocacy under matched initial conditions.
For a fixed initial target fraction $x_0=n_Z/N$, the susceptibility is
\[
\chi(x_0)=\mathbb E[\Delta x\mid {\rm ADV},x_0]
          -\mathbb E[\Delta x\mid {\rm NOOP},x_0].
\]
Holding the epistemic composition fixed, advocacy produces a positive shift
toward the controller target at every tested state:
\[
\chi(0.25)=0.163,\qquad
\chi(0.375)=0.100,\qquad
\chi(0.50)=0.104.
\]
The corresponding $95\%$ matched-pair bootstrap intervals are
$[0.119,0.202]$, $[0.060,0.144]$, and $[0.060,0.148]$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:susceptibility}a). Thus the controller has a measurable causal
influence even when the initial knowledge distribution is held fixed. The
response is largest at the lowest tested target fraction and does not increase
with additional pre-existing support over the tested range.

Microscopic controlled transitions provide a direct calibration of the
finite-compliance description. Pooling the state-matched controlled updates
gives an effective directional affinity $h_{\rm eff}=3.83$ and kinetic
compliance $\gamma_{\rm eff}=0.372$. Substituting these into
\[
\chi_{h,\gamma}(x)=
[\sigma(h)-x]\left[1-\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{N}\right)^b\right]
\]
gives the dashed finite-compliance reference in
Fig.~\ref{fig:susceptibility}a. Because $h_{\rm eff}$ and $\gamma_{\rm eff}$ are estimated from the same microscopic trajectories, this is a calibrated reference rather than a parameter-free prediction. It nevertheless shows that separating directional bias from kinetic compliance removes much of the discrepancy with the idealized copying limit.

The exact theory also clarifies the role of compliance itself
(Fig.~\ref{fig:susceptibility}b). Changing $\gamma$ rescales the magnitude of
the response while leaving the zero-response set point
$x^\star=\sigma(h)$ unchanged. This separation is useful empirically:
$h$ controls which direction is favored,
whereas $\gamma$ controls how often a controlled opportunity actually produces
a revision.

\subsection{State-local dynamics reveal where control is expressed}

The longer stochastic-feedback experiment gives access to the trajectory-level
state dependence of control. Figure~\ref{fig:landscapes}a shows the mean signed
round-to-round drift $\mathbb E[\Delta x\mid x,b]$ for $q_c=12$. Because the
controller advocates an incorrect target, the uncontrolled epistemic dynamics
usually drive $x$ downward. Increasing $b$ makes this correction progressively
weaker, particularly at low and intermediate target support. This quantity is
descriptive rather than causal: the matched experiment above remains the clean
measurement of $\chi$.

The controller action also leaves a strongly state-dependent information
signature. We measure the reduced-state conditional mutual information
\[
T_\pi(n)\equiv I(U_k;n_{k+1}\mid n_k=n),
\]
the action-to-population information induced by the chosen feedback policy
\(\pi\). Here \(U_k\) is the policy-selected controller action and
\(n_{k+1}\) is the population state after actuation.
The empirical excess above a within-state permutation null is shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:landscapes}b. At small $b$ the signal is weak, whereas at
$b=18$ a clear interior band appears. The exact finite-state theory produces
the same basic geometric mechanism (Fig.~\ref{fig:landscapes}c): local
information is high only where the action is not already deterministic and
where the advocacy and NoOp transition kernels are sufficiently separated.
The support map in Fig.~\ref{fig:landscapes}d is included because the higher
state bins are visited less often and should not be interpreted with the same
confidence as densely sampled cells.

The robust conclusion is that control information is not uniform over population state space and stronger actuation creates an informative interior region. We do not interpret the precise ridge location as a phase boundary because $n_Z$ is only a coarse-grained state.

\subsection{Feedback gain concentrates rather than simply increases information}

The theory makes a more specific prediction about the feedback policy. The
gain $\beta$ controls how sharply the controller switches between advocacy and
NoOp after sensing. At small $\beta$, the action remains uncertain over a wide
range of states; at large $\beta$, the policy becomes nearly deterministic
except close to its switching threshold. Consequently increasing $\beta$ does
not monotonically increase $T_\pi(n)$. Instead, the informative band narrows and
moves toward the decision boundary (Fig.~\ref{fig:beta}a).

The state-averaged theory curves make the same point in one dimension
(Fig.~\ref{fig:beta}b): for fixed actuation budget, larger $\beta$ can reduce
the average action-to-population information even though the controller is
more decisive. This separates controller \emph{decisiveness} from controller \emph{information transmission} and suggests a focused validation rather than a broad search: $\beta\in\{1,4,8\}$ is sufficient to test whether the empirical information band sharpens as predicted.

\subsection{Information--response efficiency increases with actuation}

The information--response efficiency asks how much of the controller-dependent
change in the full next-state distribution is expressed as a coherent shift of
the mean target fraction. Using the bounded quantity defined in
Section~\ref{sec:feedback_efficiency}, we report the finite-horizon
information--response efficiency $\eta_{\rm IR}^{(H)}$, obtained as the ratio
of the accumulated occupancy-weighted Pinsker response terms to the accumulated
state-local action-to-population information over the evaluation horizon. The
empirical values are small in absolute terms but systematic across the resource grid
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiencies}a). They range from approximately $0.4\%$ to $2.5\%$ of
the rigorous Pinsker bound and increase with $b$ for every sensing budget. At
$q_c=12$, for example,
\[
\eta_{\rm IR}=0.0043,\;0.0135,\;0.0228
\qquad\text{for } b=6,12,18.
\]
Thus stronger actuation does more than increase the size of the population
response: within this operating regime it also makes a larger fraction of the
action-dependent distributional change visible as coherent mean motion. The
small absolute values should not be interpreted as energetic losses; the
Pinsker inequality supplies a conservative information-theoretic ceiling and
need not be tight for these transition distributions.



\subsection{Thermodynamic efficiency exposes the sensing--actuation trade-off}

The single-affinity path identity leads to a bounded thermodynamic control
efficiency without introducing an opposing load. Removing the transient system
entropy term from the finite-time path irreversibility gives
\[
\Sigma-\Delta S_{\rm sys}=hJ_c+I(n_k;Y_k),
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{
\eta_{\rm th}
=
\frac{hJ_c}{\Sigma-\Delta S_{\rm sys}}
=
\frac{hJ_c}{hJ_c+I(n_k;Y_k)}
\le 1.}
\]
The numerator is the affinity-weighted target-directed current; the denominator
is the non-storage part of the finite-time thermodynamic accounting. Thus
$\eta_{\rm th}$ asks what fraction of the sensing-plus-actuation expenditure is
expressed as directed population motion. Appendix~\ref{app:thermodynamic_efficiency_derivation}
gives the complete path-measure derivation.

For empirical trajectories, we report the finite-horizon thermodynamic
efficiency $\eta_{\rm th}^{(H)}$. The affinity-weighted controlled current and
scalar target-sensing information are accumulated cycle by cycle, and the
efficiency ratio is formed only after this accumulation. We therefore do not
average the cycle-wise efficiencies, and we do not compute sensing information
from a single pooled occupancy distribution.

Figure~\ref{fig:efficiencies}b shows the exact single-affinity prediction for
the same sensing budgets used experimentally, with $h=2$, $\gamma=0.75$,
$\beta=4$, $\theta=1/2$, and $p_0(n)=\mathrm{Binomial}(24,0.25)$. At fixed
$q_c$, the efficiency rises with actuation because the controlled current grows
while the sensing information is fixed by the sensor channel. At fixed $b$,
larger sensing budgets lower the efficiency unless the additional information
produces a commensurate increase in current. For $b=18$, for example,
\[
\eta_{\rm th}=0.985,\;0.964,\;0.930
\qquad\text{for }q_c=6,12,18.
\]
The result is therefore not that sensing is intrinsically undesirable, but that
information that does not change the action-relevant population response is a
thermodynamic overhead in this control objective.

The empirical sensing measurements support precisely this mechanism
(Fig.~\ref{fig:efficiencies}c). Increasing $q_c$ substantially raises
$I(n_k;Y_k)$, whereas the calibrated controlled current changes much less across
the same resource grid. Combined with the close agreement between the measured
current and the finite-compliance current reported above, this yields a direct
design prediction: in the present regime, adding actuation improves both
$\eta_{\rm IR}$ and $\eta_{\rm th}$, whereas additional sensing is efficient
only when the policy can convert it into additional directed current.

Taken together, the results support a single resource-conversion picture of
feedback control. Susceptibility identifies where the population can be moved;
state-local information identifies where the action is dynamically visible;
$\eta_{\rm IR}$ quantifies how coherently that information appears as mean
motion; and $\eta_{\rm th}$ quantifies how efficiently the finite-time
sensing--actuation budget is converted into affinity-weighted directed current.
In the present regime, actuation is the dominant useful resource, while sensing
displays clear diminishing returns. The theory additionally predicts that
feedback gain $\beta$ should reshape, rather than simply amplify, the
information channel, providing a focused next validation of the framework.
