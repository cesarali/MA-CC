\paragraph{Purpose.}
This note fixes a minimal classical controlled-imitation model that stays close to
Irisarri et al.~\cite{irisarri2026} while matching the intended slower feedback
timescale of the multi-agent experiment. One population round contains $N$
microscopic focal-update attempts. The controller measures and decides only once
at the beginning of the round. If it chooses \Adv, exactly $b$ of the $N$
microscopic update positions are selected uniformly at random in advance and use
a controlled social-interaction kernel. No further sensing or controller decision
occurs within the round.

\section{Uncontrolled microscopic imitation}

Consider $N$ agents with binary opinions $A$ and $B$, and let
\[
n\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
\]
denote the number of $A$ agents. Irisarri et al.~use the reversible reaction
structure
\begin{align}
qA+B &\xrightleftharpoons[a_1]{h_1} (q+1)A, \label{eq:r1}\\
A+qB &\xrightleftharpoons[a_2]{h_2} (q+1)B. \label{eq:r2}
\end{align}
Reaction~\eqref{eq:r1} contains conformity $B\to A$ with strength $h_1$ and
anticonformity $A\to B$ with strength $a_1$; reaction~\eqref{eq:r2} contains the
opposite pair.

We use sampling of distinct peers without replacement. Define
\[
G_q(m)=\frac{\binom{m}{q}}{\binom{N-1}{q}},
\qquad
G_q(m)=0\ \text{for }m<q,
\]
the probability that all $q$ sampled peers belong to a specified set of $m$
eligible agents.

The continuous-time rates in Irisarri et al. can be converted to a discrete
update-attempt kernel by a common factor $\eta>0$ chosen small enough that all
transition probabilities are valid. Equivalently, the off-diagonal probabilities
are $(\eta/N)$ times their mesoscopic rates. This common factor fixes only the
microscopic clock and does not change forward/backward rate ratios.

For one ordinary microscopic update attempt,
\begin{align}
K_0(n+1\mid n)
&=
\eta\,\frac{N-n}{N}
\left[
h_1G_q(n)+a_2G_q(N-n-1)
\right], \label{eq:k0plus}\\
K_0(n-1\mid n)
&=
\eta\,\frac{n}{N}
\left[
a_1G_q(n-1)+h_2G_q(N-n)
\right], \label{eq:k0minus}\\
K_0(n\mid n)
&=
1-K_0(n+1\mid n)-K_0(n-1\mid n). \label{eq:k0stay}
\end{align}
All other entries vanish. These are the discrete-attempt counterparts of the
birth--death rates in Eqs.~(3a)--(3d) of Irisarri et al.~\cite{irisarri2026}.

\paragraph{Reaction-resolved detailed balance.}
The reaction labels must be retained. For reaction~1,
\[
\frac{K_0^{(1)}(n+1\mid n)}
     {K_0^{(1)}(n\mid n+1)}
=
\frac{h_1}{a_1}\frac{N-n}{n+1}.
\]
Since
\[
S_n^{\mathrm{int}}=\ln\binom{N}{n},
\qquad
S_{n+1}^{\mathrm{int}}-S_n^{\mathrm{int}}
=
\ln\frac{N-n}{n+1},
\]
we recover
\[
\ln
\frac{K_0^{(1)}(n+1\mid n)}
     {K_0^{(1)}(n\mid n+1)}
=
S_{n+1}^{\mathrm{int}}-S_n^{\mathrm{int}}
+\ln\frac{h_1}{a_1}.
\]
Reaction~2 has the analogous relation with bias $\ln(a_2/h_2)$. This is the
generalized local-detailed-balance structure used by Irisarri et al. to define
reaction-resolved currents and trajectory entropy production.

\section{Controlled microscopic update}

Take the controller target to be $Z=A$. At a controlled microscopic position,
one of the $q$ social-influence slots is replaced by an external advocacy message
for $A$. The remaining $q-1$ peers are sampled ordinarily.

Because one influence slot is guaranteed to be $A$, an all-$B$ influence group is
impossible. Therefore reaction~2 cannot fire at an $A$-controlled position.
Reaction~1 remains possible, but only $q-1$ ordinary peers must now be $A$. Hence
\begin{align}
K_1^A(n+1\mid n)
&=
\eta\,\frac{N-n}{N}\,
h_1G_{q-1}(n), \label{eq:k1plus}\\
K_1^A(n-1\mid n)
&=
\eta\,\frac{n}{N}\,
a_1G_{q-1}(n-1), \label{eq:k1minus}\\
K_1^A(n\mid n)
&=
1-K_1^A(n+1\mid n)-K_1^A(n-1\mid n). \label{eq:k1stay}
\end{align}

The controller changes \emph{exposure}, not the focal agent's action. Thus an
$A$-advocacy event may induce either conformity $B\to A$ or anticonformity
$A\to B$. This mirrors the LLM setting, where an advocacy message can be
accepted or rejected by the focal agent.

Importantly, the controlled kernel preserves the same reaction-level
local-detailed-balance form:
\[
\frac{K_1^A(n+1\mid n)}
     {K_1^A(n\mid n+1)}
=
\frac{h_1}{a_1}\frac{N-n}{n+1},
\]
because the same factor $G_{q-1}(n)$ appears in the forward and reverse
transition. Hence
\[
\ln
\frac{K_1^A(n+1\mid n)}
     {K_1^A(n\mid n+1)}
=
S_{n+1}^{\mathrm{int}}-S_n^{\mathrm{int}}
+\ln\frac{h_1}{a_1}.
\]

\section{Round-level feedback with an exact intervention budget}

Let $k$ index population rounds. At the start of round $k$, the state is $n_k$.
The controller acts once:
\[
n_k \longrightarrow Y_k \longrightarrow U_k,
\qquad
U_k\in\{\Noop,\Adv\}.
\]
If $U_k=\Noop$, all $N$ microscopic positions use $K_0$, so
\[
R_0(m\mid n)=\left[K_0^N\right]_{mn}.
\]

If $U_k=\Adv$, exactly $b$ of the $N$ positions are selected uniformly at random
in advance. The selected positions use $K_1^A$ and the rest use $K_0$. This
defines an exact finite-budget round kernel $R_1$.

To compute it without enumerating the $\binom{N}{b}$ schedules, augment the
within-round state by $(r,j)$, where $r$ microscopic positions have elapsed and
$j$ controlled positions have already been used. Conditional on $(r,j)$,
\begin{align}
\Prob(C_{r+1}=1\mid r,j)
&=\frac{b-j}{N-r},\\
\Prob(C_{r+1}=0\mid r,j)
&=\frac{N-b-r+j}{N-r}.
\end{align}
Let $f_{r,j}^{(n)}(\ell)$ be the probability of population state $\ell$ after
$r$ microscopic updates, starting the round at $n$, with $j$ interventions used.
Initialize
\[
f_{0,0}^{(n)}(\ell)=\mathbf{1}[\ell=n].
\]
Then
\begin{align}
f_{r+1,j+1}^{(n)}(m)
&\mathrel{+}=
\frac{b-j}{N-r}
\sum_{\ell}
f_{r,j}^{(n)}(\ell)K_1^A(m\mid\ell),\\
f_{r+1,j}^{(n)}(m)
&\mathrel{+}=
\frac{N-b-r+j}{N-r}
\sum_{\ell}
f_{r,j}^{(n)}(\ell)K_0(m\mid\ell).
\end{align}
After $N$ positions,
\[
\boxed{R_1(m\mid n)=f_{N,b}^{(n)}(m).}
\]

\section{Measurement and soft feedback policy}

The controller samples $q_c$ agents without replacement at the beginning of the
round. If $Y_k=y$ is the number of sampled agents supporting $A$,
\[
S_{q_c}(y\mid n)
=
\Prob(Y_k=y\mid n_k=n)
=
\frac{\binom{n}{y}\binom{N-n}{q_c-y}}
     {\binom{N}{q_c}}.
\]
Using the soft threshold policy,
\[
\pi(\Adv\mid y)
=
\sigma\!\left[
\beta\left(\vartheta-\frac{y}{q_c}\right)
\right],
\qquad
\sigma(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}},
\]
where $\vartheta$ is the controller threshold. Therefore
\[
p_1(n)
\equiv
\Prob(U_k=\Adv\mid n_k=n)
=
\sum_y S_{q_c}(y\mid n)\pi(\Adv\mid y).
\]

The action-averaged round kernel is
\[
R(m\mid n)
=
[1-p_1(n)]R_0(m\mid n)+p_1(n)R_1(m\mid n).
\]
For an irreducible time-homogeneous closed-loop chain, the stationary law
$\rho$ satisfies
\[
\rho(m)=\sum_n \rho(n)R(m\mid n),
\qquad
\sum_n\rho(n)=1.
\]

\section{Exact round-level controller-to-population information transfer}

Under this round-level Markov description, the one-step directional information
from controller action to next population state is
\[
\mathcal{T}_{U\to N}
=
I(U_k;n_{k+1}\mid n_k).
\]
At stationarity,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal{T}_{U\to N}
=
\sum_{n,u,m}
\rho(n)\,p(u\mid n)\,R_u(m\mid n)
\log
\frac{R_u(m\mid n)}
     {R(m\mid n)}
}
\]
in nats per population round, or in bits if $\log_2$ is used. Equivalently,
\[
\mathcal{T}_{U\to N}
=
\sum_n \rho(n)
\sum_u p(u\mid n)
\KL\!\left(
R_u(\cdot\mid n)\,\Vert\,R(\cdot\mid n)
\right).
\]
Thus the controller transfers information when its action selects a population
transition law that is distinguishable from the action-averaged law.

Horowitz and Sandberg~\cite{horowitz2014} use transfer entropy in the opposite
feedback direction---system to measurement device---for information-acquisition
bounds. The quantity above is instead the controller-to-population
\emph{actuation} direction. This distinction should remain explicit in the later
thermodynamic derivation.

\section{Annealed approximation to the exact budget}

The exact model fixes the controlled-position count to $b$. A convenient
approximation independently controls each microscopic position with probability
\[
c=\frac{b}{N}.
\]
Then
\[
K_c=(1-c)K_0+cK_1^A,
\qquad
R_1^{\mathrm{ann}}\approx K_c^N.
\]
This approximation matches the mean intervention fraction but not its
fluctuations: the exact model has exactly $b$ controlled positions, whereas the
annealed model has a binomial intervention count with variance $Nc(1-c)$.
Therefore it should be checked against the exact model for both transfer entropy
and current/activity fluctuations.

\section{Minimal next calculation}

For the first theory calculation, use the symmetric Irisarri model
\[
h_1=h_2=h,\qquad a_1=a_2=a,
\]
so that
\[
\chi=\theta_{\mathrm{Irisarri}}=1,
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{h}{a}.
\]
For the sampling-with-replacement large-$N$ model analyzed by Irisarri et al.,
the symmetry-breaking critical value is
\[
\lambda_c=\frac{q+1}{q-1},
\qquad q>1.
\]
The immediate calculation is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
    \item construct $K_0$ and $K_1^A$ for chosen $(N,q,\lambda)$;
    \item compute exact $R_0$ and finite-budget $R_1$;
    \item solve for the closed-loop stationary law $\rho$;
    \item evaluate $\mathcal{T}_{U\to N}$ exactly;
    \item compare with the annealed approximation;
    \item only then derive the large-$N$/Fokker--Planck limit.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implementation note.}
Reaction identity should be logged in the classical simulator. Irisarri et al.'s
trajectory thermodynamics is reaction resolved: distinct mechanisms can produce
the same visible jump $n\to n\pm1$ while carrying different affinities. The
occupation trajectory alone is therefore insufficient for the later entropy
production and current decomposition.
