\begin{abstract}
This report describes two completed Santa Fe sweeps and a separate sample-size
calibration. It records the exact simulation and analysis parameters, summarizes
the observed finite-population outcomes, gives equations suitable for a
mean-field approximation, and embeds every generated PNG in an indexed atlas.
The model is synthetic and makes no LLM or provider calls. The information
statistics are descriptive; they do not establish a causal controller effect.
\end{abstract}

\section{Microscopic model and update order}
There are $N=24$ agents and $F=10$ binary-signed facts. In each episode, seven
facts have weight $+1$ (truth) and three have weight $-1$, shuffled across fact
identities. Each fact starts with three distinct random holders. An agent's
initial vote is drawn from its private evidence, without sampled messages.
Truth is vote $+1$; the controller advocates target vote $-1$.

At every round there are $N$ asynchronous microscopic updates. Each slot picks
one focal agent uniformly \emph{with replacement}. The focal agent independently
retains each active fact with probability $\rho$, samples $q=3$ messages without
replacement from the \emph{previous} board, and acquires any facts attached to
those messages. If $A_i$ is its active fact set after this update and $w_f$ is a
fact's signed weight, its evidence signal is
\begin{align*}
e_i&=\begin{cases}|A_i|^{-1}\sum_{f\in A_i}w_f,&|A_i|>0,\\0,&|A_i|=0,\end{cases}\\
s_i&=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{m\in\text{sample}}v_m,\\
\Pr(v_i=+1\mid e_i,s_i)&=\sigm(\beta_e e_i+\beta_s s_i),
\qquad \sigm(z)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-z}}.
\end{align*}
The agent posts one public vote and one randomly selected active fact, if any.
The $N$ posts made during the round form the closed board. After all updates,
the controller senses 12 of these posts without replacement
($\texttt{sensing\_fraction}=0.5$) and observes their target-vote share $Y_t$.
It draws $U_t\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\sigm[8(0.5-Y_t)])$. If $U_t=1$ and the
budget is positive, it appends $b$ target-vote recommendations with \emph{no
facts} to the next board. No recommendation is posted at $b=0$, even if the
policy draw says act. The previous board remains fixed during the next round's
$N$ updates; the new board replaces it only at round close.

The exact integer budget is $b=\operatorname{round}(24b_{\rm frac})$. The sweep
uses $b=0,3,6,9,12,15,18,21,24$. Board size after a round is $24+bU_t$;
controller posts can change both the mean sampled vote and the fraction of
sampled messages that carry facts. The controller never directly changes an
agent's private facts. Round zero records the initialized population and
board; rounds 1--30 record the evolving state.

\subsection{Information analysis and scheduler settings}
Current and next target counts are exact integer counts; fact-coverage
coordinates are discretized into six bins. The pooled actuation statistic is
$I(U_t;X_{t+1}\mid X_t)$ for $t=1,\ldots,29$. The additional variants condition
on binned mean agent fact coverage, binned population fact coverage, or both.
The latter population coordinate occupies the shared estimator's \texttt{phi}
slot as a synthetic alias; it does not have HiddenBench phi semantics.
Sensing MI is also computed for rounds 1--30. The recipe requests nine round
statistics: sensing MI, target sensing MI, sensor/action MI, population,
target, truth, mean-coverage-conditioned, population-coverage-conditioned,
and jointly coverage-conditioned actuation CMI.

The shared estimator uses 200 whole-episode bootstrap resamples and 999 null
draws per available pooled/per-round estimate, 95\% confidence level, and
analysis seed 20260925, with four analysis processes per task. The actuation
null resamples actions from the recorded policy probability; its finite-draw
$p$-value is $(1+\#\{T^*\ge T\})/(1+999)$. A separate legacy bootstrap
(1000 draws, seed 43) and a separate legacy permutation null (1000 draws,
seed 42) are both disabled. Histogram computation is disabled, though the
configuration retains \texttt{save\_null\_histograms: true}. The configured
sample-size section is disabled in both sweeps. Simulation and information
arrays each request four CPUs and throttle at eight tasks; they request 8G
for one hour and 12G for eight hours respectively. Finalizers request two
CPUs, 32G, and two hours. These are execution settings, not scientific
coordinates.

\subsection{Memory, coverage, and target share}
Across all five beta pairs and nine budgets, mean final individual fact
coverage rises from 0.241 at $\rho=0.4$ to 0.996 at $\rho=1.0$; mean final
population fact coverage rises from 0.441 to 1.000. In the same equal-cell
average, final target share changes from 0.440 to 0.422. Thus memory strongly
changes the epistemic state while the aggregate target-share response is much
smaller and depends on beta regime and budget. These cell averages are not a
thermodynamic order parameter or evidence of a sharp transition at $N=24$.
For example, at $b=24$ the social-dominated target share is 0.585 at
$\rho=0.4$ and 0.614 at $\rho=1.0$, while the evidence-dominated values are
0.355 and 0.337. The complete rho-by-budget maps are in the figure atlas.

\subsection{Controller information and action support}
At positive budget, all 440 expanded-sweep cells have both recorded action
values, so pooled action CMI is defined. With 999 policy-null draws and an
uncorrected $p<0.05$ threshold, the number of detectable cells varies with
the conditioning set:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrr}\toprule
Conditioning & Detectable/440 & Mean CMI minus null (bits)\\\midrule
Plain & 318/440 & 0.035\\
Mean & 294/440 & 0.032\\
Population & 301/440 & 0.032\\
Both & 274/440 & 0.028\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
These are counts across a parameter sweep, not a multiple-testing-adjusted
discovery count. The plain variant is detectable in 318/440 positive-budget
cells; the joint coverage variant in 274/440. Corrected pooled CMI is often
positive but can be slightly negative after null subtraction. The minimum
null $p$-value is 0.001 at 999 draws. At $b=0$ the physical action never
occurs, so actuation CMI, susceptibility, and its $p$-value are undefined.
The effective-action field, not the policy's latent $U$ draw, is used.
Conditional response $\chi$ is the observed within-bin difference in next
target share between action states, weighted by supported strata. Feedback
confounding remains possible, so neither $\chi$ nor CMI is a causal effect.

\subsection{Sample-size calibration}
The separate baseline-pair ($\beta_e=1.0,\beta_s=1.3$), $b=6$ calibration
simulated 400 reference episodes at each of $\rho=0.75$ and 1.0. At each rho,
200 repeated subsamples were drawn at $n=10,20,30,50,75,100,200$. Each
repetition requested 100 episode-bootstrap and 199 policy-null draws for
plain target CMI. Detection means unadjusted null $p<0.05$. Selected results:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{rrrr}\toprule
$n$ episodes & Detection at $\rho=.75$ & Detection at $\rho=1$ & Note\\\midrule
10 & 0.050 & 0.040 & 200 subsamples/rho\\
50 & 0.195 & 0.385 & 200 subsamples/rho\\
100 & 0.395 & 0.790 & 200 subsamples/rho\\
200 & 0.880 & 1.000 & 200 subsamples/rho\\
\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
At $n=100$, detection is 0.395 at rho 0.75 and 0.790 at rho 1.0 in this

The 200 subsamples repeatedly reuse one 400-episode reference population per
rho, so these fractions quantify detection in that reference, not universal
power. They also do not calibrate the other nine rho values.

\section{A mean-field approximation to try}
Let $p_t$ be the population truth-vote fraction, $h_{f,t}$ the probability
that a randomly chosen agent holds fact $f$, and $m_t$ the mean vote among the
$N$ regular posts on the closed board. Tracking $m_t$ separately from $p_t$
matters because the $N$ posts come from asynchronous draws with replacement.
Given an effective controller action $u_t\in\{0,1\}$, an approximate mean
vote of the next board is
\[
S_{t+1}\approx\frac{N m_t-bu_t}{N+bu_t},\qquad
\Pr(u_t=1\mid y_t)=\sigm\!\left[8(0.5-y_t)\right],\qquad
y_t\approx\frac{1-m_t}{2}.
\]
The last approximation neglects sensor sampling noise. A useful first
closure can retain its binomial or hypergeometric distribution rather than
replacing $Y_t$ by its mean, because the sigmoid is nonlinear.

Suppose $M_{f,t}$ of the $B_t=N+bu_t$ previous-board messages carry fact $f$.
The exact probability that a three-message sample contains fact $f$ is
\[
A_{f,t}=1-\frac{\binom{B_t-M_{f,t}}{q}}{\binom{B_t}{q}},\quad q=3.
\]
An independence closure for one focal update is
\[
h_{f,t}^{\rm new}\approx \rho h_{f,t}+
  (1-\rho h_{f,t})A_{f,t}.
\]
This is the right place to model fact dilution by controller posts: they
increase $B_t$ but never $M_{f,t}$. It is also necessary to track at least the
distribution of evidence balances, not only mean coverage, because
$\mathbb E[\sigm(\beta_e e+\beta_s s)]$ generally differs from the sigmoid
at the means. With a closure for that expectation, an approximate population
equation over one round is
\[
\frac{dp}{d\tau}=\mathbb E_{e,s}\bigl[\sigm(\beta_e e+\beta_s s)\bigr]-p,
\quad 0\le\tau\le1,
\]
where one unit of $\tau$ means $N$ asynchronous slots. If the update target
probability were constant during a round, this reduces to
$p_{t+1}\approx e^{-1}p_t+(1-e^{-1})\mathbb E[\sigm(\beta_e e+\beta_s s)]$.
That constant-field approximation is only a starting point: the code changes
facts and votes during the round, even though it samples a fixed previous
board. A stronger closure tracks vote/fact correlations and the distribution
of regular posts as well as $p_t$ and $h_{f,t}$. Fit or check such a closure
against the saved time-resolved trajectories, not just the final heatmaps.

\section{Interpretive limits}
The finite design has $N=24$ and 100 episodes per cell. No thermodynamic
limit, critical exponent, or sharp phase boundary was estimated. The
rho-by-budget heatmaps are empirical response maps. Plot colors should be
compared against the tables because each image can have its own color scale.
Pooled CMI is calculated from pooled observations, not by averaging per-round
estimates. Fact-coverage bins are six-level coarse variables, and the
population coverage alias is specific to this synthetic adapter. The null
test samples actions from the recorded stochastic policy; it does not
randomize the controller itself. Statistical significance across hundreds
of cells is unadjusted for multiplicity. The calibration reference is small
relative to a universal power study. These limits matter when using the
results to choose a mean-field closure or a future experiment.
